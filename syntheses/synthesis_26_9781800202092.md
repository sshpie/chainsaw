## One-liner

A hands-on, tool-by-tool field manual for monitoring, threat-hunting, assessing, attacking (red team / pentest), and incident-responding an Industrial Control System network, built entirely around a reproducible Company X/Y/Z lab on the Purdue model.

## Thesis

ICS/OT security is fundamentally about visibility and validation, not prevention alone: because industrial environments invert the IT CIA triad (Availability over Integrity over Confidentiality), are full of unpatchable, open-by-design, decades-old equipment, and cannot tolerate downtime, defenders must continuously SEE and VERIFY their security posture through layered passive monitoring, active interrogation, threat hunting, threat intelligence, and periodic assessments, then prove exploitability with red-teaming and pentesting against a SAFE testbed approximation rather than live production, and finally prepare a disciplined incident-response program for when (not if) something gets through. The defender must be right every time; the attacker only once.

## Core ideas

1. The CIA triad is INVERTED for OT: Availability (uptime) is the top priority, then Integrity, then Confidentiality - the exact reverse of IT - which reshapes every defensive and offensive decision (e.g., a SYN-flood DoS is 'by far the biggest risk' because ICS gear is already underpowered and overloaded).
2. The Purdue / ISA-95 reference model (Levels 0-5 with an Industrial DMZ at Level 3.5) is the organizing skeleton for the whole book; the IDMZ brokers all IT/OT traffic and segmentation into enclaves/choke points is the primary architectural defense.
3. Three escalating monitoring postures exist and must coexist: PASSIVE (architecture-based, never touches devices - Zeek/Suricata/Security Onion on SPAN ports), ACTIVE (interrogating/scanning devices, which is risky on fragile OT), and THREAT HUNTING (proactively assuming compromise and searching for it).
4. Threat information is not threat intelligence: raw IOCs become intelligence only after context, relevance, and analysis are added; the book operationalizes this with AlienVault OTX, MISP, honeypots (Conpot/T-Pot), GreyNoise, VirusTotal, Censys/Shodan, and glue tools like combine, ElasticIntel, and zeek-otx.
5. Threat hunting is driven by falsifiable hypotheses - the book teaches exactly three: (1) malware command-and-control beaconing, (2) malicious/unwanted applications running, and (3) suspicious external connections - each with a concrete detection workflow.
6. Assessments form a ladder of increasing rigor and confidence: gap analysis -> vulnerability assessment -> risk assessment -> penetration testing; a pentest validates the LIKELIHOOD term of the risk equation (Risk = f(Severity, Criticality, Likelihood, Impact)) so mitigation can be prioritized by real exploitability and ROI.
7. Serious ICS attacks follow a TWO-PHASE ICS Cyber Kill Chain (SANS adaptation of Lockheed Martin's Intrusion Kill Chain): Phase 1 = espionage/foothold in the enterprise network (Planning, Preparation, Intrusion, Management/Enablement, Sustainment); Phase 2 = the ICS attack itself (Planning, Validation, ICS attack) - and pentests should be modeled around these four high-level steps.
8. ICS environments are EASY targets by design: open-by-default protocols, default/guessable passwords (Password1, Password123), no built-in AAA / encryption / logging, proprietary and unpatched systems (Conficker/MS08-067 and WannaCry/MS17-010 still live in plants), low cybersecurity awareness, and thin production margins that starve security budgets.
9. NEVER pentest or aggressively scan live production OT - it is the ultimate active activity (scanning, fuzzing, exploiting, stressing fragile devices). Build an APPROXIMATION testbed (p2v conversions, cloned VMs, exact firmware revisions, spare PLCs) so you take a representative sample of each unique device and attack THAT, not the running plant.
10. Incident response in OT is a documented PROGRAM, not improvisation: a periodic Preparation process plus an event-triggered Handling process, mapped to the SANS six steps (Preparation, Identification, Containment, Eradication, Recovery, Lessons Learned), with defined roles (OT Administrator, Technology Owner, Incident Lead, Incident Reporter), four severity tiers, severity-tied communications windows, and a ready jump bag.
11. Compromising an engineering WORKSTATION that has the controls programming software (Step 7, RSLogix, FactoryTalk) is more valuable and easier than attacking PLCs directly - this is exactly how Stuxnet worked, modifying centrifuge PLC code via the infected programming station.
12. The defender's asymmetry: 'as defenders of security we need to be right all the time while an attacker only needs to be right once,' and the average company takes 200+ days to detect a compromise - which is the entire justification for continuous monitoring and threat hunting.

## Techniques and methods

- Passive monitoring deployment: stand up Security Onion 2 with Zeek (full packet/flow capture), Suricata/Snort (signature IDS), and Wazuh HIDS agents, fed from SPAN/MIRROR ports or an ESXi promiscuous-mode vSwitch, with logs landing in the ELK stack (Elasticsearch/Logstash/Kibana) for SIEM-style analysis.
- Beaconing detection workflow: in the connection logs filter on protocol (TCP), filter OUT local destination IPs, then filter on payload size band (~10 to 10,000 bytes) to surface regular-interval C2 callbacks; confirm with RITA (Real Intelligence Threat Analytics) 'show-beacons', reading the score (e.g., 0.996), connection count, average bytes, and interval (e.g., 600s / 10 min).
- Memory forensics with Volatility on a .vmem image inside a REMnux VM: imageinfo -> pslist/pstree -> sockets/connections -> dlllist/dlldump/svcscan -> yarascan, to expose a malicious DLL loaded from c:\windows\temp\ hiding inside svchost.exe as a fake service.
- Malware analysis - static (strings, pedump for imports/exports, IDA Pro disassembly) and dynamic (OllyDbg debugging plus INetSim to simulate the C&C server so the sample beacons in a sandbox); cross-referenced to Sikorski's Practical Malware Analysis.
- Endpoint/host hunting at scale: YARA rules with CrowdResponse/yara_scanner, munin for VirusTotal hash lookups, Autoruns/autorunsc.exe for persistence, and remote execution via PsExec, Invoke-Command, wmic, and tasklist across the domain.
- Log-based detection tuning: enable Sysmon and PowerShell Script Block Logging (event code 4104) to decode obfuscated 'powershell -EncodedCommand' payloads, and watch failed-login IDs 4625/4771/539 and user-creation IDs 4720/4738/4732/4728/4722.
- Risk assessment procedure: asset identification, system characterization, vulnerability identification (Nessus/Qualys/Rapid7), threat modeling, CVSS-based risk calculation via Risk = f(Severity, Criticality, Likelihood, Impact), then mitigation prioritization.
- Red team tradecraft: scheduled Nmap/Ndiff diffing, Hydra RDP brute force, PoshC2 implants for persistence/arpscan/portscan/SharpSocks SOCKS proxy, Responder NTLMv2 poisoning, John the Ripper cracking, evil-winrm + proxychains pivoting, Mimikatz, BloodHound AD mapping, multi-hop RDP, and ControlFlash firmware upload to a target PLC - all Living off the Land where possible.
- ICS/OT-specific probing: Nmap enip-info script on TCP 44818/2222 for Rockwell device/firmware, PLCSCAN (TCP 102 S7comm and TCP 502 Modbus), Modbus-cli to read/write coils and registers (e.g., read %M1 5, write coil 1 = 0), cisco-torch for Cisco IOS, Grassmarlin to graph a network from a Wireshark PCAP.
- Credential attacks in the OT domain: password spraying with Kerbrute (Kerberos pre-auth, near-stealth, finding five accounts sharing Password123 in 0.022s), then dumping ntds.dit via ntdsutil.exe, extracting hashes with impacket-secretsdump, and cracking with Ophcrack rainbow tables or John the Ripper.
- Firmware and hardware reversing: binwalk -e to extract a 1756-L75 firmware .dmk, identify VxWorks/ARM, load into IDA Pro; and JTAG hardware hacking via JTAGulator/JTAG Finder/JTAGenum to find TAP pins, then XJAnalyser for firmware extraction.
- Internet-exposure self-assessment with Shodan: look up your ASN/public range on ipinfo.io, run net:<CIDR> queries narrowed by vendor (e.g., 'rockwell', 'enbt OR en2t', '1761-NET-ENI/D'), and treat any exposed control device as already compromised.
- Building the safe testbed: ESXi on Dell Precision 7920s, Cisco 3750s for VLANs/ACLs/SPAN, pfSense IT and OT firewalls, p2v/clone of production hosts, exact firmware on spare PLCs, emulated Siemens (python-snap7), Modbus (pymodbus), CIP (cpppo), and OPC-UA (python-opcua) stacks with spoofed vendor MAC addresses, a simulated Chinese attacker on 222.222.222.0/24 (Kali at 222.222.222.222), and the iprip.dll beaconing malware installed as a service.

## Memorable

- The lab pulls roughly 1,000,000 events every 24 hours (~250,000 in 8 hours, 31,416 connections); a TCP filter cut it to 15,276 and the size filter to just 86 candidate beacons.
- RITA show-beacons output line: 0.996,172.25.100.220,222.222.222.222,594,862,265108,432,600,318 - a 0.996 confidence beacon, 594 connections, avg 862 bytes, on a 600-second (10-minute) interval to the China IP.
- The planted malware is iprip.dll loaded from c:\windows\temp\ (a highly suspicious path), disguised as 'Intranet Network Awareness (INA+)' service IPRIP inside svchost.exe (PID 1040), beaconing to very-malicious-website.com/serve.html every 600 seconds; its user-agent is built as '<hostname> Windows XP 6.11' = 'HMI-2 Windows XP 6.11'; MD5 ebcf387056a5ae4e1c62f5b595b65250.
- On weak passwords: Hydra cracked RDP creds reports_user/Password1 ('It is baffling to me how modern Windows installs by default still allow simple passwords such as Password1'), and Kerbrute found five accounts sharing Password123 in a mere 0.022 seconds.
- Other cracked creds in the Company Z attack: admin1 = SouthEastWlcThunder-2020 (John the Ripper) and mgibson = Password123 ('Mark surely doesn't pick smart passwords!'), enabling a triple RDP hop and a ControlFlash custom-firmware upload to the Test_Left PLC at 172.25.200.11.
- The defender's asymmetry, stated plainly: 'as defenders of security we need to be right all the time while an attacker only needs to be right once,' alongside the fact that the average company takes 200+ days to detect a compromise.
- On internet exposure: a Shodan search for 'enbt OR en2t' surfaces internet-attached Rockwell PLC network cards (mostly on cellular providers), prompting the rule 'If your stuff shows up on Shodan.io, you should consider yourself compromised!' - the author uses a random 142.46.240.0/21 with 262 results and finds an exposed 1761-NET-ENI/D at 142.46.240.78.
- Stuxnet as the canonical workstation-pivot lesson: it infected the engineering station running Siemens Step 7 and modified the centrifuge PLC code from there, never attacking the controllers directly.
- Modbus write demo from Modbus-cli: 'modbus write 172.25.200.21 1 0' flips coil 1 to 0, verified by reading it back - a one-line demonstration that an open Modbus device has no authentication.
- Incident-response severity drives communications timing precisely: low-severity within a week, medium within 24 hours, high within 8 hours - and the high tier pulls in IT IR, safety/security, senior leadership, and legal counsel.
- The jump bag spec is exhaustive and concrete: contact list, blank forensic-capacity storage, trusted-tool removable media, OS/app install media, asset inventory and firewall-rules copies (each <=12 months old), printed IR forms, chain-of-custody forms, evidence tags, and even the specific cables (regular Ethernet, crossover, and a Cisco console cable).
- The author's real lab is named hardware: two Dell Precision 7920 towers off eBay (64 GB RAM, dual Xeon bronze/silver), Cisco 3750 switches for VLANs/SPAN, leftover Rockwell PLCs, VMware ESXi, pfSense firewalls, and Windows XP SP2/SP3 HMIs - eBay called out as 'my best friend for the lab equipment.'

## Curriculum fit

Sits at the operational core of an ICS/OT defensive-and-offensive security track and maps directly to the DCWF AI/cyber work roles relevant to : T&E (672) for the assessment/pentest/red-team chapters and Risk/Ethics (733) for the risk-assessment and incident-response program design. It is the practitioner companion to standards-level material - it operationalizes ISA99/IEC 62443, NIST CSF, NIST SP 800-61 (IR), SP 800-84 (test/exercise programs), and the Purdue model with copy-pasteable commands and a full reproducible lab, so it slots in AFTER a foundations course (what ICS/Purdue/protocols are) and BEFORE or ALONGSIDE deep-dive specializations in OT malware reversing, ICS DFIR, or vendor-specific exploitation. For an OSINT/recon-focused researcher it is strongest as the monitoring + threat-hunting + Shodan-self-assessment reference and the kill-chain-modeled pentest playbook; it is intentionally hands-on and lab-driven rather than theoretical, and Chapter 15 (formal risk-assessment mechanics) must be pulled from the external GitHub PDF to complete the assessment-methodology unit.
