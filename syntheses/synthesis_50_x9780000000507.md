# 50. Physical Fault Injection and Side-Channel Attacks on Mobile Devices

## What this book actually is

Not an O'Reilly tradebook: it is the Shepherd, Markantonakis, van Heijningen, Aboulkassimi, Gaine, Heckmann and Naccache academic survey (Royal Holloway / Netherlands Forensic Institute / CEA / French Gendarmerie, EU Horizon 2020 EXFILES project), ~28 dense pages consolidating 50+ physical fault-injection (FIA) and side-channel (SCA) papers from 2009-2021 on mobile system-on-chips. It is forensics-and-evaluation framed (lawful evidence recovery from locked phones is the stated motive) but reads as an offensive map. The card-grade value is the taxonomy, the cost matrix, the 10-goal impact index, and a six-part methodological-honesty critique that is a verification checklist in disguise.

## Structure and where to read

- **Section 2 (Background, lines ~234-627):** mobile SoC anatomy; GlobalPlatform TEE two-world model (REE vs TEE, EL3 secure monitor, SMC); ARM TrustZone NS-bit, TZPC/TZASC controllers; Apple Secure Enclave (SEP, sepOS L4 fork, PKA, memory protection engine, AES-XEX+CMAC); Google Titan M; authenticated secure boot with OTP eFuses, SHA-256/RSA-2048/ECDSA; key management (HUK/KDF hierarchies, Android Keymaster/StrongBox); FDE (dm-crypt, DEK wrapped by PIN-derived KEK) and FBE (per-file FEKs). Read this for the trust-boundary vocabulary.
- **Section 3 (Fault Injection, lines ~628-1144):** the FIA taxonomy. Transient vs permanent, invasive vs non-invasive. Generic setup (controller PC, oscilloscope, generator, trigger, DUT, Figure 6). Invasive: focused-ion-beam (gallium, ~2.5nm, $100k+, IC microsurgery to read fused keys), heavy-ion microbeam (single-event upsets), laser FIA (Vasselle bypassed Android secure boot by flipping the TrustZone NS-bit in the SCR with 95% accuracy via 978nm IR laser). Non-invasive, the practical core: voltage glitches (Barenghi underfeeding to swap ARM instruction encodings; Timmers privilege-escalation via LDR/LDMIA instruction corruption and skipped setresuid checks; NCC MediaTek secure-boot skip; VoltJockey cross-core fault via software-controlled PMIC/DVFS); clock glitches (CLKSCREW overclocking the secure world from non-secure via DVFS; PLL circuits frustrate external-clock attacks); heating; and EMFI (BADFET second-order pulsed EMFI defeating secure boot; Gaine EM strcmp/su bypass via libpam; Trouchkine fault-location characterization on registers/pipeline/MMU/cache).
- **Section 4 (Side-Channel, lines ~1146-1570):** observe rather than perturb. Power analysis (SPA/DPA/template; Hamming-weight correlation; very few mobile attacks because supply pins are occluded by BGA, so most power work is on MCUs/SBCs; ML/DL key recovery, ASCAD dataset, VGG-16 surprisingly effective). EM analysis, the one approach used extensively on phones (non-invasive): Aboulkassimi JVM-distortion handling, Genkin ECDSA from iPhone EM, One&Done single-trace constant-time RSA, Bukasa/Leignac TrustZone TEE-vs-REE leakage, Vasselle first PoP attack recovering boot-ROM BL1 decryption key, Lisovets extracting the iPhone 4 256-bit UID key after 300M traces. Screaming Channels (Camurati) recovers AES at 10m via analog/digital crosstalk into a radio transceiver. Acoustic and temperature SCAs noted but not viable on modern mobile.
- **Section 5 (Evaluation, lines ~1576-2731):** the comparison criteria, the **10 attack goals** (TEE code exec; TEE and REE AES/RSA/ECC key recovery; load unauthorized TAs; bypass secure boot; bypass run-time verification; load data into RAM; key resetting; instruction-level reverse engineering), Tables 3-4, and the six honesty challenges: (1) attacks target unprotected reference impls, almost none with known countermeasures, none against Apple SEP or Titan M co-processors; (2) single-target bias (80%+ FIA, 60%+ SCA papers test one device; 96% accuracy dropped to 2.45% on a sibling board); (3) almost no PoP attacks; (4) chosen-plaintext/ciphertext infeasibility (sub-0.1% fault rates, 1-500M traces); (5) inaccessible BGA hardware and no OEM datasheets, forcing black-box methods and a security-by-obscurity warning; (6) device-destruction risk from over-glitching and decapsulation.

## The load-bearing takeaways for our work

Attacker cost is quantified (the precision/cost/skill/damage matrix), impact is enumerated (the 10 goals), and the survey's own skepticism about idealized single-target results is the same verification ethic we run: a scan or a paper produces a candidate, reproducibility across platforms produces a finding, and absence of a published attack is never proof of safety.
