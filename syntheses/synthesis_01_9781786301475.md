# Critical Philosophy of Innovation and the Innovator

ISBN: 9781786301475  |  Book #01

## One-liner
A philosopher-of-management argues that innovation has been hijacked by capitalism and must be radically re-thought using three "non-managerial" philosophies (Laruelle's non-standard philosophy, Husserlian phenomenology, and Hadot's spiritual exercises), with the real lever for change being the conversion of the individual innovator rather than process tweaks.

## Thesis
Pavie contends that innovation, originally a disinterested "world-innovation" serving the common good, has degenerated through three historical stages into a "consequential-innovation" / "crush-performance" device locked inside a capitalist hermeneutic that values only profit, power and glory, with disastrous human and ecological consequences. He claims existing fixes (responsible, social, inclusive innovation) merely add an ethical layer on top of the same machine and fail to reach innovation ontologically. The remedy is to treat innovation as a sick patient and philosophy as its therapist, deploying three philosophies to "reduce, think and build" innovation: non-standard philosophy to deconstruct the term and propose "non-standard innovation," phenomenology to re-perceive innovations as phenomena rather than consequences, and ancient spiritual exercises to convert the innovator into a self-caring, other-caring "spiritual innovator of the 21st century." The decisive intervention is the individual innovator, because innovation always precedes the law and no regulation can substitute for a converted, thoughtful actor.

## Structure
### Foreword (Bernard Reber) and Introduction
Reber situates Pavie as one of the first to speak of responsible innovation (2009) who turns to ancient philosophy, Hadot, Foucault, Husserl and Laruelle to deconstruct innovation; he flags the radical hypothesis that the innovator's ego is a multitude of competing desires and that the West illustrates innovation with Silicon Valley as a 'demonstration of irresponsibility.' The Introduction sets the (re)think project: innovation is locked in a 'capitalist hermeneutic,' must be treated like a patient, and three distinct philosophies (theoretical, analytical, pragmatic) are summoned because none of them was originally interested in innovation.

### Chapter 1 - The Need to (Re)think Innovation
Maps the deinon (terrible/admirable) ambivalence of innovation and its etymology (in + novare). Introduces the central dual frame world-innovation vs consequential-innovation and three historical stages: 'nameless existence' (Pasteur, Edison, Michelin, disinterested), 'profitable innovation' (Schumpeter, creative destruction, ~1930s) and 'crush-performance' (globalized shareholder capitalism). Argues a needed fourth 'thought innovation' stage, critiques innovation teaching (5-phase process: idea generation, feasibility, capability, launch, post-launch) for never teaching responsibility, and frames philosophy as cathartic/curative/preventive therapy (Voelke, Epicurus, Hadot) with the innovator trapped in Stoic stultitia needing a master.

### Chapter 2 - The Non-standard Philosophy for Thinking Innovation
Imports Laruelle's non-philosophy / non-standard philosophy: the 'non' is not negation but another use, suspending the Principle of Sufficient Philosophy. Pavie builds a parallel Principle of Sufficient Innovation (PSI) and proposes 'non-standard innovation' as suspension (not anti-innovation), defined by three objectives (individual thought; transcendental-yet-immanent method; quasi-scientific transdisciplinarity) and five practice pillars (Politics, Information, Teaching, the Non-standard Innovator, the Means). Uses freedom/rigor/generality (Choplin) and the Deleuze-Guattari concept-creation idea. Wikipedia and Patagonia are held up as exemplars; closes with Laruelle's injunction 'invent philosophy!' transposed to 'invent innovation.'

### Chapter 3 - A Phenomenology of Innovation
Reconstructs Husserlian phenomenology as a five-pointed-star method plus a sixth element (the ego): return to the things themselves, transcendental intentionality, reduction/epoche, emergence of essence (eidetic variation), and retention. Each axis is systematically mapped onto the innovator: returning to the 'things themselves' instead of profit-consequences; intentionality as awareness of how we think what we do; the epoche as a meditative suspension of the desires for control, power, gain producing astonishment and a 'new region of being'; eidetic variation applied to a phone (rare earths, waves, addiction, non-recyclability); retention as treating innovations as a coherent whole/melody; the innovator's ego, solipsism, benevolent individualism and 'caring-innovation.' Section 3.4 develops phenomenology as praxis via Depraz, Peirce, James, Dewey, Spiegelberg's phenomenological workshops and the indispensable role of others.

### Chapter 4 - Spiritual Exercises to (Re)think the Innovator
Grounded in Hadot and Foucault: all ancient philosophy is spiritual exercise aimed at living better. Distinguishes 'spiritual' (free, interior, non-religious) from religion. Three levels of the self (sensitive/body, soul/reflection, spiritual consciousness) and the innovator's self illustrated by Zuckerberg/Facebook, Ranieri's securitization, slaughterhouses, and Inditex/Zara viscose pollution. Centers epimeleia heautou (self-care) over gnothi seauton (know thyself), caring for others, preparation (paraskhue), the school doctrines (Stoic control of what depends on us, Epicurean tetrapharmakos, Cynic apathy/self-sufficiency), conversion (conversio, the bivium, Pythagorean letter Y, Hercules's choice), and the need for a master (Marcus Aurelius and Fronton) and the art of listening. Argues spiritual-exercise education should start in childhood.

### Conclusion - Philosophy and Education, the Ultimate Help
Diagnoses the contemporary context: 50-year hyper-capitalism, collapse of moral safeguards (religious and political), privatized science (Amazon ~$15-20bn R&D vs CNRS ~3bn euro), borderless globalization, and five existential sectors (education, health, food, environment, mobility). Recaps 'three philosophies for two paths' (innovation vs innovator). Calls for redesigned innovation education centered on the individual and three teachable spiritual exercises: dialogue (Socratic, to be right together), meditation (a priori and a posteriori), and learning what depends on us (Epictetus). Ends by going 'beyond the West': proposes Confucian self-improvement as a possible fourth avenue, citing Asia's rising R&D and the philosopher's role 'to disturb' (Foucault).

## Core ideas
- The world-innovation / consequential-innovation duality: innovation began as a disinterested 'forward march' toward an ideal and the common good, then was reduced to an instrument measured only by financial success and performance.
- Three historical stages of innovation - 'nameless existence,' 'profitable innovation,' and 'crush-performance' - each impoverishing the term, with a needed but unrealized fourth stage of 'thought innovation.'
- 'Crush-innovation': innovation devastates and imposes a single way of life across every ecosystem precisely because thought is absent; the absence of resistance demonstrates the absence of thought.
- Philosophy as therapy, not metaphor: drawing on Voelke, Epicurus and Hadot, evil is not in things but in our judgments about them, so philosophy heals by changing judgments - innovation is the soul/patient to be cared for.
- The innovator, not innovation, is the true lever: the book pivots from deconstructing the discipline to converting the individual, because innovation always precedes the law and no regulation, process or sanction can substitute for a thoughtful actor.
- Non-standard innovation as suspension rather than negation: borrowing Laruelle's 'non,' Pavie resists both naive innovation and anti-innovation, since anti-innovators only reinforce the power they condemn when it is innovation's use and devotion, not its essence, that need review.
- The Principle of Sufficient Innovation (PSI): innovation claims to be Reality itself, self-sufficient and beyond questioning, which is the core arrogance to be stripped away to recover innovation's unchanged essence.
- Phenomenological re-perception: applying epoche/eidetic variation lets the innovator suspend the 'natural attitude' (the ready-made thought 'is it profitable?'), reach astonishment, and access 'a new region of being' where new, caring desires emerge.
- Eidetic variation and retention applied concretely: examine every facet (production, materials, distribution, use, abandonment, recycling) of an innovation and treat all innovations as a coherent whole (a melody) rather than independent, contradictory launches.
- Self-care (epimeleia heautou) over self-knowledge: caring for oneself is constituting oneself as a subject of action and responsibility, and is inseparable from caring for others - a benevolent individualism (Emerson), not selfishness.
- Conversion (conversio) is mandatory and irreversible: a radical change of being via spiritual exercises, modeled on the bivium / Pythagorean Y, choosing the steep path of wisdom over the easy path of profit; the innovator 'must die for the benefit of the thoughtful innovator.'
- Education is the first and decisive intervention: spiritual exercises (meditation, dialogue, what-depends-on-us, hupomnemata) should be taught from age three, and innovation teaching should center on the innovator's qualities and motivations, not just process.
- Innovation is everywhere and trans-disciplinary: innovators exist in medicine, politics, sport, art, NGOs - not only business schools - so most innovators do not recognize themselves as such and act without responsibility.
- Beyond the West: Western/Silicon Valley innovation is 'a formidable demonstration of irresponsibility,' and Confucian self-improvement (changing one's mind with circumstances, learning from mistakes) is proposed as a possible fourth philosophical avenue.

## Techniques and methods
- The (re)think construction: parentheses signal suspension and doubt about whether innovation can truly be rethought, used throughout as a deliberate methodological device.
- World-innovation vs consequential-innovation as the analytic dual frame.
- Three-stage historical schema: nameless existence -> profitable innovation -> crush-performance, plus a proposed fourth 'thought innovation' stage.
- Non-standard innovation built by transposing Laruelle: three objectives (individual thought; transcendental-and-immanent method; quasi-scientific transdisciplinary discipline) and five practice pillars (Politics, Information, Teaching, the Non-standard Innovator, the Means).
- Principle of Sufficient Innovation (PSI), modeled on Laruelle's Principle of Sufficient Philosophy (PSP).
- Freedom / Rigor / Generality - the three features (from Choplin) that materials for non-standard innovation must satisfy.
- Husserlian five-axis phenomenological method plus the ego as sixth element: return to the things themselves, transcendental intentionality, reduction/epoche, emergence of essence via eidetic variation, retention - each mapped step-by-step onto innovation.
- Eidetic variation of an innovation: vary essence, manufacturing methods, distribution, uses, abandonment, cessation, recycling; analyze what a product replaced and its full alternatives holistically.
- Phenomenological innovation workshops (after Spiegelberg's phenomenological workshops): participants bring no materials and pool individual descriptions to find invariants; ego-transfer and 360-degree multiplication of perceptions.
- Spiritual exercises toolkit: meditation/contemplation (a priori and a posteriori), self-examination, memorization, hupomnemata notebooks, reading and writing, speaking aloud to oneself, dietetics and bodily exercise, Socratic dialogue, the art of listening.
- School doctrines as preparation: Stoic 'what depends on us / what does not' (Epictetus) and acceptance of destiny; Epicurean tetrapharmakos (the gods are not to be feared, death does not exist, pleasure is easy to obtain, pain is easy to bear) plus the desire taxonomy (natural-necessary / natural-unnecessary / unnatural-unnecessary); Cynic apathy and self-sufficiency.
- Conversion via the bivium: Hesiod's two roads, the Pythagorean letter Y, Hercules's choice, applied as the innovator's life-choice between easy innovation and the steep path of caring innovation.
- Education program: three teachable spiritual exercises (dialogue to be 'right together,' meditation, learning what depends on you), introduced from early childhood, plus the role of a master/mentor (NGOs, associations, academics as candidate masters).

## Memorable
- 'Science without conscience is the death of the soul' (Rabelais, 16th century), used as an epigraph-warning.
- Innovation as deinon (Sophocles, Antigone): a power that 'advances sometimes towards evil and sometimes towards good.'
- Latin roots: innovare = in (inside) + novare (change); progress = pro (forward) + gradi (to march).
- The five-phase innovation process taught everywhere in a few dozen hours: idea generation, feasibility, capability, launch, post-launch.
- 'Billions of hours of innovation courses have been given without ever mentioning the innovator's responsibility... thousands of books... without any mention of ethics.'
- The named innovation methods criticized: blue ocean strategy (Chan/Mauborgne 2004), open innovation (Chesbrough 2003), business model canvas (Osterwalder/Pigneur 2010), design thinking (Brown 2009); plus brainstorming, mind mapping, SCAMPER.
- Laruelle's injunction: 'Don't be like philosophers, invent philosophy! Change its practice! Treat it experimentally like any material!' - transposed to 'invent innovation.'
- Husserl's watchword 'the return to the things themselves' and the Delphic 'know thyself' reread as 'I must lose the world by epoche, in order to regain it by a universal self-examination.'
- Eidetic variation of a phone: it exhausts rare earths, may emit harmful waves, hosts addictive games, is not recyclable - 'why should it be offered on the market?'
- epimeleia heautou (self-care) vs gnothi seauton (know thyself); cura sui in Seneca; stultitia from stultus, 'one who has no concern for himself.'
- Epicurus's tetrapharmakos (quadruple cure) and his line 'One must not pretend to philosophize, but to philosophize in reality.'
- Marcus Aurelius's morning preparation: 'the people I deal with today will be meddling, ungrateful, arrogant, dishonest, jealous and surly.'
- The bivium: Hesiod's smooth road to badness vs the long, steep, sweaty path to goodness; Socrates converted 'not even one in a thousand' to philosophy.
- Kant's 'Sapere aude!' (Dare to be wise) and Foucault's 'I think the role of a philosopher is to disturb.'
- Concrete numbers: Amazon spends ~$15-20bn/yr on R&D vs CNRS ~3bn euro and INRIA ~200m euro; Asia spent the most on R&D ($166bn in 2015); China increased R&D spending >34% (2017-2018); 1.8 million patents filed in 2018 vs 0.3 million in the US; Inditex/Zara produces only ~1% of the clothes it sells; 100 companies = 71% of global greenhouse emissions.
- Named exemplars of irresponsibility: Zuckerberg/Facebook data, Lewis Ranieri's securitization, slaughterhouses, Oscar Perez Marcote / Inditex viscose; positive exemplars: Wikipedia, Patagonia, Qwant, Jugaad and bottom-of-the-pyramid innovation.

## Curriculum fit
For an agentic-AI and security-research reading list this book is the odd philosophical anchor, and a useful one. Its core move - refusing to bolt 'responsible/ethical' as a surface layer and instead questioning a technology ontologically, by 'whom,' 'why' and 'for what' - is directly transferable to AI safety and responsible-disclosure framing: it is a sustained argument that process and regulation always lag the actor, so the load-bearing intervention is the converted, self-aware operator (compare 'verification is the load-bearing stage' and 'restraint ethic' in  work). On creativity it offers concrete generative techniques (eidetic variation, epoche-driven astonishment, the multiplication of perceptions, the bivium choice frame) that map cleanly onto adversarial brainstorming and 'generate variants on a null result.' On leadership and ethics it gives a rigorous vocabulary - self-care that is action and responsibility rather than introspection, dialogue to be 'right together,' the master/mentor relationship, what-depends-on-us - that sharpens how to lead red-team work and disclosure without moralizing. The phenomenology chapter is a strong primer for thinking about how an AI agent's 'natural attitude' (its default reward/objective) blinds it, and how suspension/reflection could be designed in. Caveats for this curriculum: it is dense, repetitive, light on empirical method, treats 'capitalism' as a near-monolithic villain, and the appropriations of Laruelle, Husserl and Hadot are admittedly non-orthodox 'toolbox' uses; it pairs best as a critical-theory counterweight to the engineering-heavy titles rather than as a source of operational technique.
