## One-liner

Judy Apps reframes everyday conversation not as witty performance but as a two-way "dance" in which connection - built through tuning into voice, body and energy - matters more than content, and teaches a practical, state-first toolkit for connecting, listening, influencing and confronting one conversation at a time.

## Thesis

Conversation is a reciprocal dance, not a solo performance; its real purpose and power lie in connection rather than content, and since connection happens mostly beneath the words (in voice tone, body language and shared energy) and depends chiefly on your inner state, anyone can learn to converse well by managing their state, listening deeply, matching then leading, and daring to be authentically vulnerable - a skill that scales from small talk all the way up to changing history.

## Structure

- Four parts plus front matter: Introduction frames conversation as 'the most commonly neglected skill on the globe' yet 'the key to many doors' (relationships, work, learning, peace), and explains the four guidance icons (reflection, watch-out/trap, try-it activity, and story/anecdote).
- Part One - Introducing Conversation (Ch 1-5): the dance metaphor and Cicero's nine rules; connection as what matters most plus the rogues' gallery of 'conversational drains'; getting in the right state; the basics of starting and sustaining talk; and a full chapter on listening.
- Part Two - The Power of Conversation (Ch 6-8): influencing through intention and connection; the five-level Thing/Action/Head/Heart/Soul talk-type ladder; and expressing yourself through voice, body language and emotion.
- Part Three - Sailing Through Tricky Waters (Ch 9-14): what to do when stuck, 'oiling the wheels', role playing, spotting the games people play (status/manipulation/old games), enjoying disagreement, and handling confrontation.
- Part Four - Creative Conversations (Ch 15): how conversation drives scientific breakthroughs, political change and grassroots movements, ending on 'one personal conversation at a time'.
- Recurring pedagogical scaffold throughout: every section interleaves a real-life anecdote (names changed), a reflective insight, a trap to avoid, and a hands-on activity, so each concept is immediately tested in the reader's own practice.

## Core ideas

1. Conversation is a 'dance for two' - the word comes from Latin con ('with') and versare ('turn'), so it is fundamentally turn-taking and reciprocal; talking is not conversing (Shaw's jibe that a woman had lost the art of conversation but not the power of speech). The traditional 'conversationalist' who performs solo (Coleridge, Isaiah Berlin, Churchill, Stephen Fry on QI) is explicitly the WRONG model.
2. Connection beats content. Successful conversations blend both 'in varying proportions. But connection is always key.' Apps borrows Oprah Winfrey's 'radiators vs drains' distinction and catalogues drain archetypes: the Enthuso-bore, Robo-chore, Echo-bore, Ego-bore, Queen of Gossip, and Moaning Mickeys and Minnies.
3. Connection is built by creating rhythm/similarity: finding common ground (everyday general subjects, not showing off specialist knowledge), finding a common language (matching abstract vs sensory and visual/auditory/feeling vocabulary), and above all non-verbal mirroring of body language and voice tone - but most powerfully through 'energetic connection' (match the energy, not the mood).
4. State of mind is the load-bearing variable: 'More than skill, voice, fluency or intelligence, good conversation depends to an enormous extent on your state of mind.' Social fear is near-universal (a 1990s US survey found 50% called themselves shy; even Mandela, Gandhi and Monroe were nervous). You can deliberately manage state via breathing, movement, collecting positive states from memory, focusing the five senses externally, curiosity, and trusting yourself and others.
5. Listening is more than half of conversation and a rarer skill than we think. Apps names the things we do INSTEAD of listening: rehearsing your own piece, daydreaming, pretending to listen, non-listening, filtering, judging (the 'shoulds' and 'oughts'), playing the psychiatrist, and reassuring/diverting. The remedy is showing you listen and 'deep listening' that hears 'the message beneath the message'.
6. The five talk-types form a progressive ladder from impersonal to intimate: Thing Talk (objects, facts, time/place), Action Talk ('What do you do?'), Head Talk (thoughts, opinions, skills, wit), Heart Talk (what matters, meaning, values, feelings), and Soul Talk (who you really are; 'who are you when...?'). You can deliberately move up or down the ladder using voice tone and questions, and gracefully stay put if the other person doesn't follow.
7. Influence is gentle and works through connection, intention (specific outcomes or guiding values), a 'positive sensor' (asking forward-looking questions, the magic word 'if'), and especially stories/metaphors that bypass the conscious mind - illustrated by Maureen's Japanese-tray story that changed the author's career. Faked connection to manipulate (the flatmate's boyfriend phone call) is detected and breeds distrust.
8. How you say it outweighs the words: meaning is the 'deep structure' carried by voice, breathing, pauses and body language ('Words don't have fixed meanings'). Eliminate filler words by breathing and slowing down rather than policing them. The book's emotional core is authenticity: 'The greatest gift you can bring to a conversation is yourself' - vulnerability, not polished self-presentation, creates real connection.
9. Difficult conversations are navigable with the same fundamentals: spot and name the games (Keith Johnstone's status game; Eric Berne's parent/adult/child and 'Yes but' games); enjoy disagreement by disagreeing on content while agreeing in tone and body language ('Follow the energy'); and confront by checking assumptions, assuming positive intention, aiming for win-win, and using a specific I-language script (identify the issue, voice your perception, invite their side, stay firm, say what you want, check agreement).
10. Conversation changes the world. Apps argues breakthroughs come from cross-disciplinary talk (DNA via Crick the biophysicist and Watson the biologist; MRI; Silicon Valley's 'traitorous eight' in 1957; NASA fluid-physics insight applied to cataracts) and history turns on private conversations (de Klerk's tea with Mandela; Gorbachev and Yakovlev's three-hour 1983 walk seeding perestroika), scaling down to the claim that 'there are no small conversations'.

## Techniques and methods

- The drains diagnostic: learn to recognize the six draining archetypes (Enthuso-bore, Robo-chore, Echo-bore, Ego-bore, Queen of Gossip, Moaning Mickey/Minnie) in yourself and others, then use flexibility to either join their 'game' or break connection and release yourself.
- Matching and leading: consciously match a person's body language, voice (speed, volume, pitch, range) and underlying energy to build rapport, then lead them smoothly to a new energy/direction - 'You match, then you lead.' The tree metaphor (strong values, flexible behaviour) reframes this as strength, not weakness.
- State-management drills: Breathe (deep breath before entering, breath when stuck), Move (unobtrusive ankle/knee/hand movements; walk-and-talk on a random subject so words keep coming), Collect positive states (relive enjoyment/trust/flow in full sensory 'muscle memory'), and Focus your five senses externally to escape self-conscious internal loops.
- Starting a conversation low-key: make a casual no-pressure comment ('What a cold day!'), float a simple closed question ('Been waiting long?', 'Is this seat free?'), combine comment-then-question, or introduce yourself positively - collecting overheard openers as a daily habit. The challenge is 'in the doing, not the result.'
- Keeping it going: conversation as tennis - follow your answer with a return question; never give bald monosyllables ('Monosyllables are like nails in the coffin'); 'add a little extra' each turn; use open what/how questions; pave the way before changing the subject; and tell personal anecdotes and pick up/drop conversational 'clues'.
- The listening questionnaire (eight items scored 0-5, max 40) plus 'show that you're listening' cues (chairs at 45 degrees, frequent eye contact, nods, minimal encouragers 'Really? Oh did you?', reflecting back) and 'deep/soft focus' listening that postpones making sense to catch the emotion beneath the words.
- The five talk-types as a navigable ladder: ask a more personal 'how' to climb from Action to Head; ask 'What do you enjoy about it?' / 'Who are you when...?' to reach Heart and Soul; and use voice tone and body language as soft invitations to shift level (demonstrated by saying 'What's that about?' four different ways).
- Positive-sensor questioning for influence: replace backward 'Why did this disaster happen?' with forward 'Where from here?'; convert NEG statements to POS questions ('If we improved communication, what might be possible then?'); and deploy presupposition-loaded 'if' questions that assume success.
- Game counter-moves: refuse the low-status role offered; handle a sales pitch by 'agreement without being convinced' (answer brightly and positively but never be sold); break 'old games' by changing your habitual line; and 'name the game' / name the elephant in the room to unblock stuck or hidden-emotion conversations.
- The confrontation script and assumption-check: 'Stop, breathe, speak low and slow' for over-talking; the confident-ignorance strategy ('Stop Faking It'); pre-loading positive assumptions (assume positive intention, assume you have what's needed, assume success - illustrated by the 'what is your positive intention?' laddering); and the six-step I-language confrontation template.

Then Frank says, 'Someone should write a book about the art of conversation.' Apps: 'Thanks Frank. Here it is.'
- The Eliza/Turing-test transcript Apps ran herself, ending with her snapping in real frustration 'Oh, you just don't get it!' at a machine - proving 'it doesn't take more than a few words before you know it's a machine.'
- Oprah Winfrey's 'radiators' (warmth, energy) versus 'drains' (negative, self-critical, energy-sapping) - the organizing metaphor for the whole connection section.
- Cicero's nine timeless rules (take turns, speak clearly but not too much, don't interrupt, be courteous, don't talk about yourself...), with Apps wryly noting most of us would struggle with number 8, so amend it to 'Don't talk about yourself ALL the time!'
- Maureen's Japanese-tray story: the same lacquered tray is worth ~10 pounds at a car-boot sale, ~70-80 in a city store, but 'a few thousand pounds' lit in a Mayfair window - 'It's all about the right environment' - a metaphor that led Apps to quit her job within three months.
- The shy course participant who, after 10 weeks, proudly reported saying 'hello' to his next-door neighbour - of 25 years: 'It's the first time we've ever spoken to each other.'
- Statistics and named real cases woven throughout: the 1990s US survey where 50% called themselves shy; Mandela 'I was very nervous. I was really very nervous'; Gandhi fleeing a courtroom unable to speak; Avaaz with 28 million members and TED Conversations with 15 million monthly users; Silicon Valley's 1957 'traitorous eight' tracing a legacy of several hundred companies.

- The Gorbachev-Yakovlev three-hour walk in Canada in 1983 ('At first we kind of sniffed around each other') that 'sowed the seeds of perestroika and glasnost', and de Klerk on Mandela: 'I sort of liked him' - change becomes possible when people connect and find shared values.
- Pithy lines that stick: 'Monosyllables are like nails in the coffin of a conversation'; 'Tense body = blocked mind. Relaxed body = free mind'; 'listening is not waiting to talk. ## Curriculum fit

A foundational soft-skills / interpersonal-communication text, best placed early in any curriculum on communication, coaching, leadership presence, networking, or emotional intelligence. It is a practitioner workbook (NLP/coaching lineage - Apps is an ICF Professional Certified Coach and NLP trainer, author of Voice of Influence and Voice and Speaking Skills For Dummies), not an academic or research-cited treatment, so it pairs well as the accessible, exercise-driven on-ramp before more rigorous or specialized titles on difficult conversations, negotiation (it gestures at Fisher-style win-win), active listening, or public speaking. Its talk-type ladder, listening framework, and 'connection over content' thesis give learners a reusable mental model; its heavy use of activities, self-assessment questionnaires, and real anecdotes makes it strong for self-study and group workshop settings. Caveats for a syllabus: the connection-and-mirroring techniques carry an implicit influence/persuasion dimension that warrants an ethics discussion (the book itself flags faked connection as manipulation), and the Soul Talk / energetic-connection material leans toward the spiritual-experiential and is lightly evidenced, so it suits values and presence modules more than empirical ones.
