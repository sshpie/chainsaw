## One-liner

A hands-on, lab-driven walkthrough of a full penetration-testing engagement using Kali Linux, taking the reader from building an isolated VirtualBox lab to compromising an Active Directory forest, cracking WPA3, and writing the client report.

## Thesis

Glen D. Singh argues that ethical hacking is a teachable, repeatable methodology rather than a collection of tricks, and that anyone can learn it cheaply by building a self-contained virtual lab and walking the full attack lifecycle on machines they own. The book structures every technique around recognized frameworks (Cyber Kill Chain, MITRE ATT&CK, PTES, OWASP Top 10) and a seven-phase engagement model, insisting that legal authorization, scope, and reporting are as much a part of the craft as the exploits. Its through-line is the chained engagement: passive recon, to active scanning, to exploitation, to post-exploitation pivoting through three isolated lab networks and into a domain, demonstrating how a single foothold escalates to full domain dominance.

## Core ideas

1. The penetration test is a disciplined lifecycle, not ad-hoc hacking: the book repeatedly maps every action onto the Lockheed Martin Cyber Kill Chain (recon, weaponization, delivery, exploitation, installation, C2, actions on objectives), MITRE ATT&CK, and a 7-phase engagement (pre-engagement, recon, enumeration, vulnerability assessment, exploitation, post-exploitation, reporting).
2. Legal authorization is the load-bearing prerequisite: written permission is framed as the pentester's 'get-out-of-jail-free card,' backed by the SOW, NDA, and Rules of Engagement (RoE) - and the book explicitly tells the reader to stop and report to law enforcement if human-rights violations or a real breach are discovered mid-test.
3. A free, fully isolated virtual lab makes the whole curriculum reproducible: three intentionally separate internal networks (PentestNet, HiddenNet, RedTeamLab) are designed specifically so the reader practices pivoting and lateral movement and cannot accidentally attack systems they do not own.
4. The chained engagement is worth more than the sum of its findings: the lab is deliberately built so HiddenNet (10.11.12.0/24) is reachable only by first compromising the dual-homed Metasploitable 3 Windows host on PentestNet, teaching pivoting/autoroute as a first-class skill.
5. Active Directory is the centerpiece target, and the book treats it as the realistic path from a single workstation to domain dominance via credential theft, relaying, and Kerberos abuse rather than single CVEs.
6. Credentials, hashes, and tickets are the universal currency of post-exploitation: hashdump -> Pass-the-Hash -> NTLM relay -> Kerberoasting -> golden/silver tickets is presented as one continuous escalation story.
7. Wireless security is a generational arms race (WEP -> WPA -> WPA2 -> WPA3) where each standard closes the prior attack and opens new ones; WPA3's SAE/PMF defeat deauth-based handshake capture, forcing downgrade attacks.
8. Web application security is taught entirely through the OWASP Top 10 2021 as a checklist, with each of the ten categories mapped to a concrete exploitable challenge in OWASP Juice Shop or WebGoat.
9. Social engineering exploits seven psychological levers (authority, intimidation, consensus, scarcity, urgency, familiarity/likeness, trust) and is the human attack surface that complements the technical chain.
10. Defender empathy and reporting are part of offense: covering tracks, the importance of logging/SIEM (the A09 logging-failures lesson), and writing a report readable by non-technical executives using CVSS severity are taught as deliverables, not afterthoughts.
11. Antimalware evasion is its own sub-discipline: the book distinguishes signature-, behavioral-, and heuristic-based detection and shows encoding (shikata_ga_nai) and PE injection (Shellter) as the practical bypasses, while noting modern AV often still catches them.
12. Tooling is framework-anchored, not tool-worship: Empire is taught via the listeners/stagers/agents model, Burp via the intercepting-proxy + Repeater/Intruder model, and BloodHound via the Neo4j graph model, so the reader learns the concept beneath each tool.

## Techniques and methods

- Build isolated VirtualBox networks with VBoxManage dhcpserver add (PentestNet/HiddenNet/RedTeamLab) and deploy Metasploitable 3 via Vagrant (rapid7/metasploitable3-win2k8 dual-homed, and ub1404 Linux).
- Set up bind vs reverse shells with Netcat, then generate and encode payloads with MSFvenom using shikata_ga_nai (-i 20 iterations) and inject into a legitimate PE with Shellter to evade signature detection.
- Exploit the vsFTPd 2.3.4 backdoor on Metasploitable for a Linux foothold, and EternalBlue (MS17-010) for Windows; dump the SAM, crack LM/NTLM hashes with John and Hashcat (-m 1000 NTLM, -a 0 dictionary).
- Perform Pass-the-Hash with Impacket psexec/PTH-WinExe/xFreeRDP, then post-exploit with Meterpreter (getsystem, hashdump, incognito token impersonation, migrate, autoroute for pivoting), establish persistence, and clearev to cover tracks.
- Stand up a C2 with PowerShell Empire 5: create a listener, generate a stager, catch an agent, run Mimikatz/bypassuac/psinject, and apply a malleable profile (e.g., windows-updates) for traffic blending; drive it with the Starkiller GUI.
- Enumerate AD with PowerView (Get-NetDomain, Get-DomainSID, Get-NetUser, Get-NetComputer, Find-LocalAdminAccess, Invoke-ShareFinder) and visualize attack paths with BloodHound via SharpHound / BloodHound-Python collectors over Neo4j.
- Capture and crack NTLMv2 by poisoning LLMNR/NBT-NS with Responder (Hashcat -m 5600), and relay NTLM to SMB with ntlmrelayx.py when SMB signing is not enforced; abuse IPv6 with mitm6 to relay to LDAPS.
- Kerberoast service accounts with GetUserSPNs.py (Hashcat -m 13100), dump NTDS.dit with secretsdump, and forge persistence with Mimikatz golden tickets (kerberos::golden using the krbtgt hash), silver tickets, and the skeleton key (misc::skeleton).
- Capture a WPA/WPA2 4-way handshake with airodump-ng + an aireplay-ng deauth, then crack with aircrack-ng and a wordlist; against WPA3 use a downgrade + dictionary attack because PMF defeats deauth.
- Run web attacks through Burp Suite (FoxyProxy at 127.0.0.1:8080) using Repeater to test the ' OR 1=1; -- SQLi auth bypass in Juice Shop, Intruder + rockyou.txt to brute a security-question answer, and Decoder for Base64/ROT13; automate full DB extraction with sqlmap (--dbs / --tables / --columns / --dump) against DVWA.
- Exploit the full OWASP Top 10 as labs: broken access control (direct /administration/ access), SSRF and logging-failure lessons in WebGoat, reflected/DOM/stored XSS, and hook victim browsers with BeEF for client-side social-engineering payloads (Fake LastPass).
- Generate custom wordlists with Crunch and CeWL, run social-engineering campaigns with the Social-Engineer Toolkit (site cloner / credential harvester) and Wifiphisher (rogue AP + Firmware Upgrade page), and set up secure remote engagement access with ZeroTier (push-through VPN, up to 25 devices) plus XRDP on Kali.

## Memorable

- The opening line that frames the whole book: 'Cybersecurity is one of the most exciting and rapidly growing fields in the world.'
- Written permission is described as 'like having a get-out-of-jail-free card as a penetration tester, but this comes with a responsibility.'
- The SQLi auth bypass distilled to one payload: entering ' OR '1'='1 makes the WHERE clause always evaluate true, returning the first record - the administrator account.
- Juice Shop SQLi in practice: injecting fake@email.local' OR 1=1; -- returns an authentication token that logs you into the admin account with no valid password.
- The example NTLM admin hash used throughout the SAM/PTH material: Administrator:aad3b435b51404eeaad3b435b51404ee:e02bc503339d51f71d913c245d35b50b (the aad3b435... half is the empty-LM placeholder).
- Hashcat mode cheat-sheet embedded in the AD chapters: -m 1000 (NTLM), -m 5600 (NTLMv2), -m 13100 (Kerberos TGS / Kerberoasting).
- The golden ticket's danger: it is forged from the krbtgt hash and survives a krbtgt password change, while the skeleton key (password 'mimikatz') is lost when the DC reboots because it lives only in LSASS memory.
- WPA3 is presented as immune to classic deauth-driven handshake capture because Protected Management Frames (PMF) and SAE replace the WPA2 4-way handshake - forcing attackers to downgrade.
- The three-network lab is engineered for pivoting: HiddenNet (10.11.12.0/24) is reachable ONLY through the dual-homed Metasploitable 3 Windows host on PentestNet, so you must compromise one box to reach the next.
- The logging-failure (A09) WebGoat lesson: you forge a log line by submitting the username 'user1. Login successful for username: admin', showing how poor log sanitization fakes an admin login - then Base64-decode the admin password from the server log.
- The hacker hardware 'tool bag' shopping list: Flipper Zero, WiFi Pineapple Nano (Hak5), Bash Bunny (a Linux machine disguised as a USB stick), Packet Squirrel, LAN Turtle, and a Spacehuhn-firmware ESP8266 deauther.
- ZeroTier is characterized as a 'push-through VPN' that 'metaphorically punch[es] through a firewall,' supporting up to 25 devices on a free network for remote engagement access.

## Curriculum fit

This is the foundational, build-it-yourself entry point for a self-study offensive-security curriculum: it is the book you read first to stand up a safe lab and walk an entire engagement end-to-end before specializing. It assumes essentially no prior pentesting experience and teaches the Kali tool surface broadly rather than deeply, so pair it with hands-on platforms it explicitly recommends - TryHackMe and Hack The Box for graded practice, plus RangeForce for the blue-team counterpart - and with the vulnerable targets it uses (Metasploitable 2/3, OWASP Juice Shop, WebGoat, DVWA). For depth beyond its breadth, follow each domain with a specialist text: 'The Web Application Hacker's Handbook' or PortSwigger Web Security Academy after Chapters 16-17; 'The Hacker Playbook' / OSCP-style material and 'Active Directory Security' resources after Chapters 10-13; and the OWASP Top 10 2021 docs and SANS report-writing whitepaper (both cited in-text) for methodology and deliverables. It also maps cleanly onto certification prep for eJPT, PNPT, Security+/PenTest+, and as warm-up for OSCP. Its weakest coverage (modern EDR evasion, cloud/container pentesting, mobile) marks exactly where a learner should branch out next; treat it as the spine, not the whole skeleton.
