# 60. Emerging Trends in Applications and Infrastructures for Computational Biology, Bioinformatics, and Systems Biology

Book 60 is an atlas, not a monograph. Thirty-five chapters from the BIOCOMP 2014 and 2015 tracks (WORLDCOMP, Las Vegas), collected by Tran and Arabnia into five sections: methodology and algorithms, bioinformatics and simulation, systems biology, large-scale data analytics, and clinical applications. We treat it the way we treat a good recon corpus, as a routing surface. The value is not any single chapter's depth; it is the breadth of transferable moves and the recurring class of problem underneath them.

One class dominates: turn an intractable object into a tractable surrogate, then measure it. Chapter 8 refuses the 2^n by 2^n probabilistic-Boolean-network transition matrix and instead ranks gene influence with Boolean partial derivatives feeding a column-stochastic power iteration, PageRank on a regulatory network, O(n^2), 500 to 600 genes in minutes. Chapter 6 (REDCRAFT) pins a protein backbone to 2.084 angstrom from only N-H and Ca-Ha residual dipolar couplings across two alignment media, because the Saupe tensor makes sparse orientation constraints load-bearing. Chapter 21 (Infogenomics) reads a genome as a Shannon source, scoring entropy, KL and Jeffrey divergence, hapax and repeat indexes, and recurrence distance distributions against a Bernoullian null, with suffix arrays keeping it computable. The lesson repeats: find the polynomial-cost proxy, then anchor every reading to a random baseline.

The second thread is discipline about method choice. Chapter 5 says pick your parallelism from the memory topology, not the core count: OpenMP past ten processes on shared memory, MPICH in a ring rather than a star when distributed. Chapter 19 shows why it matters: a serial 3D cellular automaton needs roughly 200 hours for one cubic centimeter of tissue, which is the exact wall that hands the problem to Book 59 and the GPU. Chapter 9 warns that lower grammar-space entropy buys RNA-model accuracy and overfitting in the same breath, so the Typical Set Criterion has to arbitrate. Chapter 22 is the sharpest methodological caution in the volume: quartile and equal-count binning agree only near trivial residuals and disagree everywhere else, so discretization is a modeling decision to run multiple ways and compare, never a silent preprocessing step. That chapter chains cleanly out of the lobe into Book 20 on fairness, where the same binning bias produces unjust outcomes.

The clinical section earns its keep with Chapter 31: model a blood-bank workflow in YAWL, translate it with a hand-patched YAWL2DVE, encode FDA and AABB mandates as linear temporal logic, and model-check the full vein-to-vein chain in Divine over nine states, 152 predicates, and 56 constants, all inside OpenMRS. This is regulation made executable, which is why we synapse the chapter to reliability and verification work (Book 33) rather than to the biology.

Use this book as a dispatcher. When a task touches gene networks, route through Chapters 8, 12, 17, and 20 and pull Book 57. When it touches structure from sparse constraints, route through Chapters 6 and 13 and pull Books 56 and 63. When it touches classifiers on omics, route through Chapters 15, 20, and 24 and pull Books 61 and 65, then challenge their grid-search parameter tuning with the metaheuristics of Book 62. When it touches information content or randomness, route through Chapters 1, 9, and 21 and pull Books 03 and 32. The caveats are permanent: conference-volume rigor varies, every numeric constant is tuned to its dataset, and the tooling is 2014-2015 vintage. Re-verify before you cite. The chains are the payload.
