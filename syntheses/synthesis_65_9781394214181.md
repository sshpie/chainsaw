# 65. Bio-Inspired Optimization for Medical Data Mining

We read this as the applied, clinical-facing sibling to our optimization lobe. Where 62 (Meta-heuristic and Evolutionary Algorithms) and 64 (Biogeography-based Optimization) give us the machinery, 65 shows what happens when someone aims that machinery at a Parkinson's voice recording, a brain MRI, or a retinal fundus image. It is a 2024 Wiley/Scrivener edited volume, fourteen substantive chapters, mostly Indian university authors. Treat it as a field map, not a bench manual.

The through-line is one loop repeated under many animal metaphors: a population of candidate solutions, a fitness function, and an exploration-versus-exploitation balance. Genetic Algorithms supply selection, crossover, and mutation. Particle Swarm Optimization (Kennedy and Eberhart, 1995) supplies personal-best and global-best velocity updates. Ant Colony Optimization (Dorigo, 1996) supplies pheromone trails. Then the book widens the menu: Artificial Bee Colony, Firefly, Grey Wolf, Bacterial Foraging, the Dingo Optimizer (Peraza-Vazquez, 2021, population 10-50), and the Cuttlefish Algorithm. Once we see these are the same loop wearing different costumes, the book becomes a lookup table: pick the metaheuristic, plug in the fitness function, tune the handful of parameters.

Those parameters are the real payload. Ch3 wraps a GA around Parkinson's feature selection with population 100, eighteen generations, crossover 0.5, mutation 0.05, and an extra-trees fitness of ten estimators, over concrete acoustic features (jitter jitta 83.2us, jitt 1.04%, rap 0.68%; shimmer Shim 3.81%, ShdB 0.350dB) pulled with Praat and Parselmouth. Ch13 is our favorite: it flattens every ANN weight into a single chromosome. The fruit360 worked example is 108x120 + 120x80 + 80x4 = 22,880 weights per individual, six individuals = 137,280 parameters, single-point crossover 70%, mutation probability 25%, a random weight subset scaled by 2 to 5, performance plateauing at 300 generations. That is the cleanest statement in the book of how a GA escapes the local minima that trap backpropagation.

The security synapse is Artificial Immune Systems (Ch9, Ch1). AIS maps immunology onto computation with no hand-waving: antigen is the problem, antibody is a candidate, affinity is fitness, clonal selection reproduces the best, memory cells retain solutions, and self-versus-non-self discrimination IS anomaly and intrusion detection. This is the bridge from our optimization lobe to detection and defensive work, and it is why we chain to 42.

We read the accuracy numbers with skepticism. Ch6 ranks DNN 90.5% over SVM 85.3% over RF 81.9% over KNN 78.5%, which is honest. But the volume is littered with 97 to 100% claims (Ch10 vessel segmentation 97.98% on DRIVE and STARE, Ch11 RF 99.7% and J48 99.87%, cardiotocography SVM at 100%) reported on small held-out sets with no cross-validation discipline stated. Assume optimism, class imbalance, and leakage until a paper proves otherwise. This is survey-grade literature: reported, not reproduced, heavy on self-citation from a tight author cluster, with definitions repeating verbatim across chapters and one chapter (Ch2, kidney-stone phytochemistry) that has almost no machine learning in it at all.

Our operating stance: use 65 to pick an algorithm and a starting parameter set, use 55 and 56 to handle the underlying scientific data and molecular features, use 59 when the population fitness evaluation or DNN training needs GPU parallelism, and use 20 and 24 to supply the fairness and model-security rigor that Ch14 names but never engineers. The book is a doorway, not a destination. It tells us which nature metaphor to reach for and roughly how to set it up; the neighbors tell us how to do it correctly.
