# AI for Good: Applications in Sustainability, Humanitarian Action, and Health

ISBN: 9781394235872  |  Book #27

## One-liner
A field guide from Microsoft's AI for Good Lab: 28 real project case studies in sustainability, humanitarian aid, and health, bookended by an honest primer on AI's failure modes and an epilogue distilling 11 hard-won rules for partner-driven applied ML.

## Thesis
The world's hardest problems cannot be solved by AI alone; they are solved by convening multidisciplinary subject-matter experts who pair domain knowledge with proven ML techniques against real partner data. The book's purpose is to make that thesis concrete through ~28 deployed case studies while teaching the reader, with equal weight, the nine ways AI deceives its operators (bias, confusing correlation with causation, shortcut learning, gaming/Goodhart, out-of-distribution failure, weaponization, illusion of certainty, and the limits of working without domain experts). The recurring lesson is that verification, explainability, careful train/test splitting, and the "last mile" of deployment and adoption matter more than state-of-the-art model novelty. Impact in AI4G is measured not by F1 or AUC but by a partner organization's mission outcomes.

## Core ideas
- AI alone never solves a world problem; the load-bearing ingredient is convening multidisciplinary subject-matter experts who supply domain knowledge, study design, features, loss functions, and the definition of 'impact.' The SIDS/maternal-smoking and long-COVID social-determinants surprises show data can be antithetical to the hypothesis, so objectivity must be preserved.
- Predictive power is not causation. The book hammers DAGs and confounders (ice cream sales vs drownings via summer; umbrella vs rain) and repeatedly warns that even strong models surface associations, not mechanisms - explicitly restated for the pancreatic-cyst EBM ('explanations are predictive rather than causal').
- Shortcut learning is the silent killer of deployed models: the 2017 dermatology model that learned to read surgical rulers as a cancer cue, generalizing nothing. Out-of-distribution failure (a model trained on one hospital, one audio quality, one disaster type) is the most common real-world break.
- Goodhart's Law / the cobra effect governs any metric you optimize: 'When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure.' Phone-rocker devices gaming step counts is the canonical example; gaming is treated as a structural property of optimization, not a bug.
- Explainability is a deployment requirement, not a nicety. The EBM 'glass-box' model (an evolved Generalized Additive Model) beats MOCA on pancreatic cysts AND gives calibrated probability vectors clinicians can audit; 'AI techniques are useful but limited: they must be explainable to be useful.'
- Train/validation/test splitting must mirror the deployment scenario or performance estimates lie. Split building-damage data ACROSS disaster types (tornado, flood, tsunami, earthquake); split medical imaging across hospitals; split marine-mammal audio across noise conditions. Same-disaster leakage gives 'falsely optimistic performance.'
- The 'last mile' of deployment and adoption is harder than modeling and lies mostly outside the technical team's control. Three deployment modes: one-time scoring, real-time API, batch processing - each with different cost/maintenance trade-offs; open-sourcing code builds trust and drives adoption.
- Impact metrics diverge from ML metrics. F1/AUC are 'a basis for discussion'; the real KPIs are several steps removed and defined by the partner's mission. The Netherlands Red Cross cared about runtime/speed as much as accuracy; the American Red Cross cared about geographic coverage vs 'boots on the ground.'
- Privacy is an attack-surface problem, not a checkbox. The broadband study used differential privacy (Laplace Mechanism, OpenDP SmartNoise) at ZIP-code aggregation precisely because synthetic/aggregated releases are 'vulnerable to membership inference attacks' - a security-relevant framing the author names explicitly.
- Human-in-the-loop active learning beats brute-force labeling when positives are rare. Annotators were shown only satellite tiles most likely to contain whales because raw satellite data is 'far too difficult to annotate since whales are only present in a fraction' of images.
- LLMs/GPT are dilettantes by default: 'they know a little bit about a lot of topics, but they do not know a lot about a given topic.' The QuitBot team learned the dominant failure was not hallucination but insufficient domain knowledge base; the fix was iterative knowledge-base construction (11,000 QnA pairs), not bigger models.
- Comparison baselines determine whether AI 'wins.' GPT/AI should arguably be benchmarked against the average accessible provider (50th -> 75th percentile improvement helps most patients), not the world's top experts; ~4 billion people have limited care access and a 17-year research-to-practice gap exists.
- Carbon footprint and resource constraints are first-class modeling concerns in AI4G (Microsoft pledged carbon-negative by 2030); models must run on edge devices in low-connectivity regions, where compression/quantization trades accuracy and must be communicated honestly.
- Satellite-plus-AI creates a 'guidance system for civilization' / planetary Mission Control: daily global high-res imagery + supervised change detection finds 'the needle' (new deforestation roads before clearing) and turns days of human analysis into minutes for disaster response.

## Techniques and methods
- Nine-lesson AI-limitations framework (bias, correlation-not-causation, algorithmic discrimination, shortcut learning, out-of-distribution, gaming/Goodhart, weaponization, illusion of certainty, can't-solve-alone) - used as a diagnostic checklist before trusting any model.
- GPT decoder: G=Generative, P=Pre-trained, T=Transformer; transformers from Vaswani et al. 'Attention Is All You Need' (2017); next-token statistical generation explained with the 'Domesticated animals include... dogs' walkthrough.
- Evaluation-metric toolkit (Ch3): confusion matrix TP/TN/FP/FN; recall=sensitivity=TPR; specificity=TNR; precision; accuracy; AUC/ROC; F1 (harmonic mean) and F2; IoU/Jaccard; Dice Similarity Coefficient; correlation coefficient; N-fold and leave-one-group-out cross-validation; SHAP (Shapley Additive Explanations); Frechet distance.
- CLAP (Contrastive Language-Audio Pre-training) with Zero-Shot Transfer for bioacoustics; PANN and HTS-AT audio backbones; ResNet-18 encoder; multimodal contrastive learning matching audio embeddings to text prompts.
- U-Net (encoder-decoder semantic segmentation, Freiburg 2015) as the workhorse - used for bomas, glacial lakes, building footprints/density, ROP, prostate lesions; variants: Siamese U-Net (change detection on xView2/xBD), TernausNet/VGG11, DeepLabv3+, FCN, historically-guided U-Net + morphological snakes + DELSE.
- Temporal Cluster Matching (TCM) with KL (Kullback-Leibler) divergence to date land-use change (India solar farms) from Sentinel-2 imagery; Douglas-Peucker polygon simplification and SEEDS superpixels for cyclone/dwelling work.
- Explainable Boosting Machines (EBMs) as glass-box evolution of Generalized Additive Models (GAMs) producing calibrated probability vectors - the two-step pancreatic-cyst pipeline (pre-CFA and post-CFA models) benchmarked against MOCA.
- Differential privacy pipeline: Laplace Mechanism with epsilon budgets (0.1/0.2), OpenDP SmartNoise library, ZIP-code aggregation, with explicit membership-inference-attack awareness.
- Transfer learning + supervised contrastive learning: BankNote-Net (MobileNetV2, 256-dim embeddings, 24,826 images/17 currencies); BERT (110M params) transfer learning for Syrian-war event-type classification; ImageNet-pretrained ResNeXt-50 32x4d backbone with AdamW (lr 0.000562, weight decay 0.1) for population mapping.
- QuitBot NLP build process: seq2seq knowledge-base generation -> compare Azure QnA / ParlAI / DialoGPT (345M) / GPT-3 (Davinci/Curie/fine-tuned Curie) -> GPT-3.5 (175B); entropy (pertinence) + PPL (grammaticalness) automated scoring plus human raters on accuracy/repetitiveness/tone; Ada 'gatekeeper' model experiment; final dual-engine architecture (Azure QnA via LUIS for in-library, fine-tuned GPT-3.5 for out-of-library).
- Model-based geostatistics (MBG) disaggregation: combine deep-learning building-density estimates (CNN, MSE loss, piecewise smoothing, DEM/land-cover false-positive correction) with census tabulations and shapefiles to distribute people to structures across space and time.
- 11 Key Takeaways of 'Becoming Good at AI for Good' (educate partners; ongoing scoping dialogue; understand metadata/collection/privacy; catch subjective labeling early; deployment-aware data splits; incorporate domain expertise via features/model choice/regularization; consider deployment constraints before model choice; use domain-specific metrics; build human-in-the-loop/active-learning pipelines; budget long-term maintenance and frame impact as time saved; let domain experts define mission impact).

## Memorable
- George Box: 'all models are wrong, but some are useful' - invoked for the illusion of certainty and the 2008 financial crisis.
- Goodhart's Law: 'When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure' - taught via the cobra effect and phone-rocker step-count gaming.
- $35M lost in a UAE bank heist via AI voice cloning - the weaponization case study.
- 2017 skin-cancer model that learned to detect surgical rulers instead of melanoma - the canonical shortcut-learning failure.
- St. George's Hospital Medical School admissions algorithm (1980s) that encoded discrimination against women and minorities; left-handedness/lifespan study (Halpern & Coren, 1991 NEJM) as a survivorship-bias trap.
- JAMA Internal Medicine (April 2023, Ayers et al.): GPT-4 rated more accurate AND more empathetic than physicians on patient queries; ChatGPT launched Nov 2022 and drove a ~900% increase in related search.
- Otoscopy generalizability collapse: AUC 0.95 internal vs 0.76 external (single outside cohort) vs 0.96 pooled - the cleanest in-book demonstration of out-of-distribution failure.
- Pancreatic-cyst EBM could reduce unnecessary surgeries by 59% and increase correct surgeries by 7.5%; pancreatic cancer ~12% five-year survival; >75% of people over 80 have pancreatic cysts.
- QuitBot: DialoGPT (345M params) was 'limited,' GPT-3.5 (175B params) was 'far superior'; off-the-shelf LLMs are 'like dilettantes - they know a little bit about a lot of topics'; RCT N=1182, mean 72 days first-to-last use vs ~7 days for rule-based texting.
- Long-COVID study: 1.37M COVID survivors, self-controlled cohort, ICD-10 codes, BMC Public Health 2022 - found social determinants of health NOT associated with long COVID, against the hypothesis.
- Planetary scale facts: 96% of mammalian biomass is humans + livestock, 4% wildlife; Amazon stores >150 billion metric tons of carbon, home to 47M people and 400+ indigenous groups; Brazil deforestation fell 66% year-on-year, $2B+ in fines, 1,000+ machines destroyed.
- GitHub open-data study: 802 million files / 142TB; CDLA-Permissive-2.0 license; Git LFS; datasets include COCO, SQuAD, OpenAI GPT-2 dataset.
- Tools/datasets named: TorchGeo, xView2/xBD, WorldPop, Sentinel-1/2, Landsat, PlanetScope, Microsoft Building Footprints + Google Open Buildings (2.5B footprints, 92% of Level-0 admin boundaries), SpaceNet 7, Seeing AI, OpenDP SmartNoise, NewsGuard, Miburo (Microsoft Threat Analysis Center), ACLED.
- Buckminster Fuller's 'spaceship Earth' and 'you can't manage what you don't measure'; Kasie Richards (American Red Cross): work that 'would typically take us several days to process' was done in 'a more condensed period' on the 2023 Maui Lahaina fire (100 deaths).

## Curriculum fit
Strong fit across all four reading-list axes, though it is an applied-ML case-study anthology rather than a deep technical or adversarial text. For SECURITY RESEARCH: the most transferable material is the failure-mode taxonomy - shortcut learning, out-of-distribution collapse, Goodhart/gaming, and the illusion of certainty map almost directly onto red-team thinking about model robustness, evaluation gaming, and detection evasion; the broadband chapter explicitly names membership-inference attacks against synthetic/aggregated data and demonstrates differential privacy (Laplace Mechanism, OpenDP SmartNoise) as a defense, and the satellite/deforestation work is a real anomaly-detection-at-scale pipeline. The repeated insistence that 'verification and explainability are load-bearing, not optional' rhymes with the  tenet that verification, not the scan, produces findings. For AGENTIC AI: the QuitBot chapter is a candid, dated (2020-2023) build log of a production NLP/LLM agent - dual-engine routing (deterministic Azure QnA + generative fine-tuned GPT-3.5), a gatekeeper-model experiment, automated+human eval gating, and the 'dilettante' insight that domain knowledge base, not model size, was the bottleneck; Chapter 31's GPT-in-medicine framing (compare to the average accessible operator, not the top expert) is a useful evaluation-baseline argument. For LEADERSHIP: the epilogue's 11 takeaways and the Communication/Data/Modeling/Impact structure are a practical playbook for running applied-AI engagements with non-technical stakeholders, setting realistic expectations against media hype, and defining success by mission outcomes rather than vanity metrics - directly applicable to scoping and reporting client engagements. For CREATIVITY: lower density, but the cross-domain transfer moves (whale detection borrowing dog-vs-cat classifiers; CLAP zero-shot audio; chaining building-density CNNs into geostatistical population models) model the 'chain primitives across domains' habit. Net: read it once for the failure-mode chapters (1-3), the QuitBot and broadband-privacy chapters, and the epilogue; skim the 25 remote-sensing/clinical case studies for pattern recognition rather than depth.
