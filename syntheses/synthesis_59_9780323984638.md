# Programming Massively Parallel Processors, 4th Ed (Book 59)

We keep this book in the bio-compute lobe because it is the one text on our shelf that explains the machine every modern AI, scientific-simulation, and password-cracking workload actually runs on. It is the CUDA canon (Hwu, Kirk, El Hajj), and its thesis is narrow and load-bearing: on a GPU the arithmetic is rarely the hard part. The hard part is feeding 6912 cores without starving them on DRAM. Everything else follows from that.

## The one ratio that decides fate

The compute-to-global-memory-access ratio (OP/B, arithmetic intensity) rules the kernel. Naive matrix multiply is 0.25 FLOP/B and is pinned to memory bandwidth; SpMV is worse because a sparse matrix has zero reuse. A 16x16 shared-memory tile lifts matmul to 4 OP/B and roughly 6220 GFLOPS on an A100 whose peak is 19,500 GFLOPS FP32 against 1555 GB/s. The Roofline Model turns that into one picture: memory-bound or compute-bound, and by how much. We reach for this framing before we ever open a profiler.

## Patterns are the reusable capital

The middle of the book is nine parallel patterns, each with its known-good decomposition and its data-layout tradeoff: convolution (constant memory, halo tiling), stencil (register tiling, z-coarsening), histogram (privatized atomics), reduction (convergent trees, log2(N) steps), scan (Kogge-Stone vs Brent-Kung, the work-vs-step fork), merge (co-rank binary search, circular buffers), sort (radix with per-block local sort, merge for general keys), sparse matrix (COO/CSR/ELL/JDS), and graph traversal (vertex-centric push/pull, frontiers, atomicCAS). We treat these as primitives, not chapters. When a task looks like one of them, the parallelization is already solved.

## The discipline layer

Six optimizations recur (Table 6.1): maximize occupancy, coalesce, kill control divergence, tile, privatize, coarsen. Under them sit the hardware truths: warps of 32 execute SPMD in lockstep, latency is hidden by keeping warps resident (zero-overhead scheduling), and occupancy is a budget of 2048 threads and 65,536 registers per SM. Overspend registers and you fall off the performance cliff. The application chapters (MRI reconstruction, electrostatic maps, CNN inference) hammer one lesson: scatter forces atomics, gather owns its output in a register, and loop fission plus loop interchange is how we turn one into the other.

## Why it earns cross-lobe pulls

For our work the value is sideways. The occupancy and coalescing tuning here is the engine under GPU hash cracking (Book 32). The memory hierarchy it maps, shared-memory banks, constant-cache lines, coalesced bursts, is the exact surface a GPU timing side channel measures (Book 50). The NVCC to PTX to SASS pipeline is what we reverse when we analyze a shipped CUDA kernel (Book 49). Ch16 (convolution-as-GEMM, im2col, cuDNN) is the literal metal under every ML-security question (Book 24). And the reduction, scan, stencil, sparse, and graph kernels are the throughput layer under our bio-compute siblings: biological networks (57), biological computation (58), scientific data (55), structural visualization (63), and evolutionary search (62).

## How we use it

We do not copy its kernels; the book itself tells us to call cuBLAS, CUTLASS, cuDNN, and Thrust. We read it to reason about a workload's ceiling, to name the pattern, and to know what the GPU is doing when someone else's framework is on the SMs. The numbers are Ampere-specific and the API surface moves per CUDA release, so we verify limits with cudaGetDeviceProperties and the current Programming Guide before we commit.
