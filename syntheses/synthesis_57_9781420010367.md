# 57. Introduction to Biological Networks

## Introduction to Biological Networks (Book 57, bio-compute)

Raval and Ray hand us a graph-theory toolkit for molecular biology, but the parts we actually reach for are the ones that mirror how we already work: build a candidate graph from noisy signals, then spend most of the effort proving which edges are real. This book is a verification-first program dressed as network biology.

**The load-bearing lesson is our own.** High-throughput interactomes carry 45 to 90 percent false-positive rates (d'Haeseleer and Church), and the two canonical yeast two-hybrid screens (Uetz, Ito) agree on only about 150 of thousands of interactions. No topology claim survives a single experiment. The book's answer is exactly ours: integrate independent evidence, score it, set a threshold. Jansen's naive-Bayes fusion (prior odds about 1/600, accept likelihood ratio L greater than 600) is the template. When we harvest a population of candidates from one source, this is the discipline that stops us reporting confident, reproducible, wrong numbers.

**Robust-yet-fragile is an attack model.** Power-law degree distributions (yeast PPI exponent about 2.25) are robust to random node loss and fragile to targeted hub removal. The book then sharpens the target: date hubs, not party hubs, are the fracture points (Han 2004), and real pathogens evolved to hit them (T7 and phage lambda inactivate the host RNA polymerase hub; effectors of two plant pathogens converge on immune-network hubs, Mukhtar 2011). That is blast-radius reasoning, and it chains straight into how we model where a single well-placed disruption fragments a system.

**Information theory is the inference spine.** Shannon entropy and mutual information drive REVEAL and ARACNE, the latter pruning indirect edges with the Data Processing Inequality. The same math surfaces whenever we separate a real dependency from a transitive shadow.

**Null models decide significance.** The book is strict that no module or motif is significant without the right random baseline: configuration-model stub-matching or degree-preserving edge shuffles for abstract graphs (15000 shuffles in the pathogen test), but biophysics-based MpK or stickiness models for physical protein networks, because much of an observed interactome is just surface hydrophobicity. The wrong null manufactures findings.

**Modules, robustness, and evolution tie together.** Newman-Girvan modularity via the principal eigenvector of B = A - k_i k_j/(2m) recovers functional complexes; the Drosophila segment-polarity network holds its pattern across tenfold parameter swings because bistable positive feedback, not tuning, carries the function. Networks grow by gene duplication (DMC beat preferential attachment and six other models), duplication leaves evolutionary memory, and that memory lets Navlakha-Kingsford reconstruct ancient networks by stepping argmax P(G_t given G_{t-1}) backward.

**The payoff is the diseasome.** Goh's human disease network (1777 genes, 1284 diseases, 22 classes) turns disease-gene discovery into graph search: random-walk-with-restart and Laplacian diffusion kernels K = e^(beta L) rank candidates far better than direct neighbors (rank 8/100 vs 17/100), shared-gene diseases show 2-4x comorbidity, and synthetic lethality routes around undruggable targets (BRCA-PARP1, KRAS), which matters because only about 3000 of roughly 25000 human proteins have a druggable pocket.

We treat this book as a priming lens: an interactome is a population survey with a brutal false-positive floor, and every clean read has to be earned.
