# 31. Industrial Cybersecurity

**ISBN** 9781788395151  |  **Lobe** defensive

> The Purdue-model field manual: reach for it to frame any ICS/OT target in zones-and-conduits, AIC-over-CIA, asset-inventory-first defense-in-depth, and to remember you can crash a 25-year-old PLC by scanning it.

## Reach for this when

- A target sits in or behind OT/ICS infrastructure (PLC, HMI, SCADA, DCS, SIS) and you need the architectural map to know where you are and what is reachable from where
- You hit an industrial protocol with no auth or encryption (Modbus/502, S7comm/102, EtherNet-IP+CIP/44818, PROFINET) and need to confirm it is insecure-by-design, not a misconfig to report as a finding
- You are about to scan or fuzz something that might be a live controller and need the discipline reminder: never scan production OT, a network scan can crash the device
- You are classifying or scoring a finding and need to invert the priority triad: Availability first, then Integrity, then Confidentiality, because downtime/unsafe-state is the real impact in a plant
- You need to explain to a CISO why a flat IT/OT-converged network is the whole problem and what segment-and-broker (IDMZ at level 3.5) remediation looks like
- You want the offensive-to-defensive narrative spine: show how trivially the ICS falls, then justify each defensive layer

## Principles

- Insecure by inheritance: the vulnerability is the protocol design, not a patchable bug. Modbus (1979), S7comm, EtherNet-IP/CIP ship with no authentication, encryption, or integrity. Do not log 'no auth on Modbus' as a discovered vuln; it is the baseline.
- Purdue model is the spine: 6 levels (0 Process, 1 Basic Control, 2 Area Supervisory, 3 Site Operations, 4 Site Business, 5 Enterprise) split into Industrial and Enterprise zones, with the IDMZ at level 3.5 as the single brokered boundary. No traffic crosses enterprise-to-plant directly.
- AIC, not CIA: Availability > Integrity > Confidentiality for ICS. This reshapes every control and every severity score; a downtime/safety risk outranks a data-confidentiality risk.
- You cannot secure what you do not know you have: continuously-updated asset inventory (software versions, patch levels, firmware revisions) is the foundational activity, preceding scanning, patching, whitelisting, and risk assessment.
- Where you cannot patch (20-30 year lifetimes, 99.999% uptime, devices that cannot reboot), you compensate: application whitelisting (deny-all, allow by hash+signature, stops 0-days) plus firewall isolation, never leave the gap open.
- Security bubble: the oldest most-fragile devices cannot be hardened, so isolate them onto their own network (priority 1), lock down all access (priority 2), allow interaction only through a monitored conduit (priority 3).

## Moves

- Recon an ICS lab safely: Shodan + Nmap with modbus-discover.nse and enip-info, p0f for passive fingerprinting, against a representative test network only, never production
- Read a controller via Shodan the way the book's 'freebie mini pentest' did: surface a 1756-ENBT card and pull cleartext config/passwords (the book finds one literally set to 'VerySecretPa$word')
- Manipulate industrial protocols in the lab with Scapy+smod and CPPPO; exploit with Metasploit/Meterpreter/msfvenom; move laterally with Mimikatz/incognito/psexec
- Stand up the defensive stack: Cisco ASA active/standby in the IDMZ, WSUS in the IDMZ serving industrial clients on TCP 8530, AlienVault OSSIM SIEM, NXLog CE shipping, OSSEC HIDS with custom decoders, syslog severities 0-7
- Whitelisting proof move: configure Symantec CSP / AppLocker / EMET with hash+publisher rules; the book's demo omits the vendor from the whitelist so Event Viewer and netcat are blocked, proving it stops unknown executables and 0-days
- Vulnerability workflow: OpenVAS (openvas-setup/-feed-update/-start, web UI :9392) against Metasploitable, triage by severity, verify by exploiting (ncat to the port-1524 backdoor for a root shell)
- Map findings onto the Lockheed Martin 7-stage and SANS two-phase ICS Cyber Kill Chain to decide where to detect and break the chain
- Run the risk program: gap analysis (controls vs NIST 800-82) + vuln assessment + risk = likelihood x impact, scored, tracked in SimpleRisk, on a once/twice-yearly RMF improvement cycle

## Watch out

- 2017 first edition: significant tooling rot. EMET reached end of life in 2018, Symantec CSP and OpenVAS product lines/links have shifted, CVE/CVSS references are of their era. For current tooling go to book 26 (2nd edition), NIST 800-82r3, current 62443 parts. The conceptual discipline is what endures, not the product names.
- The 'never scan production' rule is load-bearing and absolute for live OT, but it can over-anchor you into passivity on IT-side or test-network targets where active probing is fine. Know which side of the IDMZ you are on before deciding scan posture.
- Do not report insecure-by-design ICS protocols (no auth on Modbus/S7comm/CIP) as discovered vulnerabilities; that is the documented baseline, not a finding. The finding is exposure/reachability across the segmentation boundary, not the protocol's lack of auth itself.
- The Purdue model is a reference architecture, not a description of reality. Real converged plants are flatter and messier than the clean 6-level diagram; treat it as the target state to measure against, not an assumption about how the target is actually built.
- The Slumbertown case study and the 'cockroaches and OPC servers' / 'VerySecretPa$word' set pieces are pedagogy. Useful as CISO-facing narrative anchors, not as data; do not cite the fictional plant as evidence.
