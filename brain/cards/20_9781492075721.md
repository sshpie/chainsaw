# 20. Practical Fairness

**ISBN** 9781492075721  |  **Lobe** ai-agentic

> The runnable adversarial-ML and ML-governance bench manual: when a target is a model (not infra), this is the toolbox for membership inference, evasion, poisoning, model extraction, fairness/bias auditing, and the GDPR/BIPA dollar figures that turn a finding into business impact.

## Reach for this when

- The attack surface IS a model, not the substrate around it. aimap/recongraph found an exposed classifier, recommender, or scoring endpoint and now you need to attack the math, not the HTTP wrapper.
- You can only touch a model's inputs/outputs (black-box, proprietary, API-fronted) and need the access-tier discipline of the pre/in/post intervention timeline to decide what is even possible.
- A finding needs a dollar figure for the CISO or disclosure: reach for the legal-exposure table (GDPR 4% turnover / 20M euro, CCPA $2,500-$7,500/violation, BIPA $1,000/violation, the $550M Patel v. Facebook settlement).
- You need to prove a privacy leak from an over-parameterized/overfit model and want the membership-inference shadow-model recipe that works WITHOUT the original training data.
- You are building or auditing an autonomous evaluator/red-team agent and need the auditing scaffolding (Raji/Google scoping-mapping-artifacts-testing-reflection, FMEA, black-box auditing for indirect influence).
- Someone hands you a single LIME or SHAP explanation as 'proof' the model is fair and you need to know why one explanation is exploitable, not evidence.

## Principles

- No Free Lunch governs everything: no single fairness metric, explainability method, or robustness defense works universally. Trusting one test is the vulnerability, not the verification.
- Privacy is probabilistic, not binary. A membership-inference attack at just-under-60% vs 50% chance is a real breach. This IS the scan-produces-candidates / verification-produces-findings distinction wearing an ML hat.
- The intervention timeline (pre / in / post-processing) is really an ACCESS-tier map: it tells you whether you can touch the data, the objective, or only the frozen outputs. Read it as an attacker deciding what is reachable.
- Interpretable does not mean correct. The GLRM 'no-cancer AND no-ADHD AND not-pregnant predicts low healthcare use' rule is readable and nonsensical. Verification and domain review stay load-bearing.
- Evasion and poisoning are two faces of one gradient vulnerability: evasion pushes one example across the boundary at inference, poisoning moves the boundary at training. Overfit/kinky boundaries are easier to attack on both.
- Markets and reputation cannot deliver fairness or privacy (privacy paradox, opacity, affected parties are externalities not buyers). Law is the most reliable arbiter, which is why the legal table is the impact-translation layer.

## Moves

- Membership inference (Shokri et al.): train N shadow models on synthetic / statistics-based / noisy real data, record (last-layer outputs, train/test label, class), train a per-class attack model. Reference run is full PyTorch on CIFAR-10, 10 shadow models, target overfit 65% train / 48% test, attack ~60%. Crucially needs no original training data.
- Decision-based evasion via IBM ART HopSkipJump (Chen et al.): query-efficient, binary-search to the boundary + Monte Carlo gradient estimate + sized jump, across L0/L1/L2/L-inf. Use when you only have query access to a black-box classifier.
- Fairness/bias audit via AIF360: load GermanDataset/CompasDataset/AdultDataset/MEPSDataset19, compute disparate impact, statistical parity difference, equal-opportunity difference; gate data with the 80%/four-fifths/adverse-impact rule plus risk difference and risk ratio.
- Black-box auditing for indirect influence (Adler et al.): measure accuracy drop after making a feature epsilon-obscure, earthmover distance, F-test equivalence. BBA.load_data('ricci'). This surfaces proxy discrimination a single feature-attribution misses.
- Explainability stack from AIX360: LIME (local sparse linear surrogate), SHAP KernelExplainer (= Linear LIME + Shapley with a background dataset), ProtoDash (MMD-minimizing weighted prototypes, features scaled to [-0.5, 0.5]). Always run more than one; Slack et al. cloak a biased classifier from both LIME AND SHAP by detecting out-of-distribution perturbations.
- Poisoning/backdoor/clean-label attacks and certified defenses: Steinhardt et al. show a 3% poison rate raised IMDb error 12% to 23%; defenses are outlier removal, domain-limited values, production noise, certified upper bounds. Packages: ART, CleverHans, Foolbox (EagerPy), secml, ML Privacy Meter.
- Translate any model finding to legal/dollar exposure using the Ch12 table; pair with the Mathur et al. seven dark-pattern categories (sneaking, urgency, misdirection, social proof, scarcity, obstruction, forced action) when the product layer, not the model, is the abuse.

## Watch out

- It is a 2020 tabular/classification book. It predates LLM-specific agentic threats (prompt injection, tool misuse, RAG poisoning, jailbreaks). Use it as the foundational adversarial-ML/fairness layer BENEATH frontier-model security work, never as a substitute. For LLM-shaped targets pair with #41/#39/#40 and current sources.
- Deliberately tool-opinionated on the IBM stack (AIF360/AIX360/ART). The concepts are durable but the exact APIs (FeatureBinarizer, LogisticRuleRegression, BBA.load_data) drift; verify against current library versions before quoting a code path as working.
- The Pleiss et al. impossibility is real: you cannot have both calibration and equalized odds in general. Do not promise a model is 'fair' on all metrics at once; that claim is mathematically unavailable and a single-metric pass is exploitable (Slack et al.).
- Fairness metrics can conflict; optimizing one disparity ratio or accuracy can silently harm another domain (privacy, minority rights, autonomy). Do not report a single ratio as 'the' fairness result.
- Legal figures are jurisdiction- and date-specific (US/EU, late 2020). Use the dollar magnitudes for impact framing, not as legal advice; reconfirm current statute and settlement numbers before putting them in a disclosure or report.
