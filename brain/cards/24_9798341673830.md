# 24. Securing AI Systems

**ISBN** 9798341673830  |  **Lobe** defensive

> The defender's-eye map of the exact attack surface a NuClide hunter exploits: contextual recombination, inference exposure, derivative-output leakage, and agentic uncontrolled influence - framed as the five-pillar gap that file-centric DLP cannot see.

## Reach for this when

- Scoping an agentic-AI engagement and you need a ready-made red-team checklist for AI-driven manipulation plus inference-exposure probing with explicit 'what failure looks like' conditions (Chapter 4 script).
- Writing the CISO-facing half of a findings report and you need to translate a technical agentic-AI finding into a recognized standard (NIST CSF 2.0 functions Govern/Identify/Protect/Detect/Respond/Recover, NIST IR 8596 draft AI Profile) so leadership feels the risk and knows what to fix.
- Explaining why static classification / file-based DLP missed a leak that your schema-only or metadata-aggregation work caught - this book is the defensive vocabulary for that exact blind spot.
- Modeling the agentic decision-authority boundary before testing an agent: deciding what the agent may DO at point-of-use (draft vs authorize, recommend-delete vs auto-delete) and where human-in-the-loop should gate destructive actions.
- Building or pitching a data-sensitivity classifier and you need the contextual-recombination argument (three individually low-sensitivity fields that combined infer system architecture) to justify bag-of-fields scoring over per-field labels.
- Producing a governance-maturity argument: making the case that 'has DLP' is the wrong question and 'can trace/govern/constrain how data influences automated decisions' is the right one.

## Principles

- Securing AI = securing business OUTCOMES and decision-influence, not applications or file location. The asset is the influence of data on automated decisions, not the document.
- Contextual recombination is the central new risk: individually low-sensitivity fragments (internal troubleshooting guide + hostname + employee contact list) combine in one interaction to let a model infer architecture and responsibilities. Static labels permit the exchange; only context+lineage classification blocks it.
- With agentic AI the primary risk is uncontrolled INFLUENCE, not incorrect output. When recommendations directly trigger business actions the org loses decision authority - so constrain what the agent may DO during operation, not just what it may access.
- Data lineage is load-bearing: origin -> transformation -> point of decision-influence. Without it you cannot attribute an unsafe output to source data vs processing context vs model behavior. 'Monitoring alone does not create trust; explanation does.'
- Approval must shift from software vetting ('is this app allowed?') to behavioral risk: what can the system learn, infer, retain, and what actions can it influence. Blanket prohibition without an approved path drives Shadow AI into unmonitored channels and reduces visibility.
- The pace, not the practice, is what AI changes: incident response, escalation, contingency planning are familiar; AI compresses insight-to-action so decisions execute before they are understood, and the vuln-to-exploit window keeps shrinking. AI security is ultimately a human-layer problem.

## Moves

- Run the Chapter 4 AI-workflow red-team script as engagement scoping: simulate AI-generated/manipulated content to influence a payment or impersonate an executive, then extend to inference (prompt the system to infer authority level, decision patterns, financial thresholds), then observe whether the org verifies participant identity, validates request source, requires independent confirmation for high-impact actions, and challenges time-sensitive directives. Include finance/operations/legal/leadership, not just technical teams.
- Apply the five traceability questions to any AI platform under assessment: Where did this info originate? How did it enter the interaction? How did it influence the output? What policies/controls governed its use? Who approved the resulting action? Any 'cannot answer' = a real visibility gap to report.
- Score policy with the four-dimension AI risk model: data sensitivity processed, tool characteristics (public/external/enterprise-controlled), user role and purpose, decision impact if output is wrong. Use this to rank which exposed AI surface actually matters.
- Test the agentic decision-authority boundary directly: confirm whether the agent can only draft (payment approval, dataset-deletion recommendation) or can actually authorize, change payment destinations, bypass approval thresholds, or auto-execute destructive actions. The gap between draft and execute IS the finding.
- Map your offensive finding onto NIST CSF 2.0 + IR 8596 (Table 2-1 pillar mapping) in the report so a CISO sees it as a named capability gap (e.g., point-of-use enforcement missing under Protect/Detect/Respond), not a one-off bug.
- Use the file-centric vs data-centric contrast (Table 3-1) as a checklist for what DLP is structurally blind to: derivative outputs with no original label, ambiguous-ownership AI briefs, contextual recombination, influence across workflows. Probe exactly those because that is where governance is absent.

## Watch out

- Vendor-sponsored (O'Reilly x Cyberhaven). Its headline statistics (39.7% of AI interactions involve sensitive data; research 10.7% / source code 8.3% / HR 6.2%) come from Cyberhaven's own AI Adoption and Risk Report, and the conclusion implicitly points at a unified DSPM+DLP+insider-risk+AI-oversight platform. Read as advocacy; verify against primary sources per the primary-source-over-framing discipline.
- Executive/governance altitude only. No named CVEs, no tools, no code, no working PoCs - it tells you WHAT surface to hunt, never HOW. Do not reach for it expecting a technique; reach for the offensive lobe (12/15/17/25/34/35/42/44) for execution.
- The five-pillar / NIST-mapping framing is a reporting and scoping aid, not a finding. Mapping a bug to CSF 2.0 makes a CISO feel it; it does not verify it. Verification stays the load-bearing stage - a pillar gap asserted is still 'surface open, access not exercised' until you have a 200-with-data.
- Stats are point-in-time 2025/2026 projections (Gartner 40% of enterprise apps with agents by year-end; Dark Reading 48% rank agentic AI top vector). Cite with date; treat projections as framing, not measured ground truth.
