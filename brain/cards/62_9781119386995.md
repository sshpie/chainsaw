# 62. Meta-heuristic and Evolutionary Algorithms for Engineering Optimization

**ISBN** 9781119386995  |  **Lobe** bio-compute

> The systematic field guide to 20 gradient-free optimizers (PS, GA, SA, TS, ACO, PSO, DE, HS, SFLA, HBMO, IWO, CFO, BBO, FA, GSA, BA, PPA, WCA, SOS, CEA), each collapsed onto one 9-step general algorithm with its pseudocode, parameters, and update equations, so you pick and code a search by structure instead of by metaphor.

## Reach for this when

- You have a black-box engineering objective with no usable gradient (nonlinear, mixed integer/continuous, constrained) and need to choose among 20 metaheuristics by structure: the per-chapter mapping table tells you which fit discrete vs continuous, single-modal vs multimodal decision spaces.
- You are implementing a metaheuristic from scratch and want the exact pseudocode plus real parameters: PSO velocity with C1=C2=2 and decaying inertia, DE mutation factor delta in [0,2], SA Boltzmann acceptance, HS HMCR/PAR, ACO evaporation rho.
- You are calibrating model parameters against observed data (Muskingum flood routing beta1/beta2/Y, groundwater, any sum-of-squared-error fit) and want to see the same least-squares problem solved by ten different algorithms.
- You need constraint handling for an optimizer and must decide between removal, refinement, and penalty functions with coefficients alpha_k, beta_k, C_k that turn a constrained problem into an unconstrained fitness.
- You must benchmark or compare optimizers honestly using NFE (number of functional evaluations) rather than run time, and report reliability as the variance of fitness across k independent runs (best/average/worst/std/CV).
- You are adversarially modeling or fuzzing an evolutionary search and want the generative core (roulette/tournament selection, one/two-point/uniform crossover, uniform/nonuniform mutation) laid out as the machinery a GA-style fuzzer runs.
- You are trading fast convergence (high selective pressure) against thorough coverage (low pressure) and need to reason concretely about premature convergence vs stagnation on a given surface.
- You want a parameter-free (SOS) or fully deterministic (CFO) optimizer to sidestep the sensitivity-analysis rabbit hole that every other algorithm in the book demands.

## Principles

- Twenty algorithms are one algorithm. Section 2.13 defines a 9-step general skeleton (read data, generate initial solutions, evaluate fitness, rename old, rank/find best, select subset, generate new, evaluate, test termination); each chapter is a mapping table (decision variable / solution / old / new / best / fitness / selection / generation) onto that skeleton. This is the book's spine and its anti-hype stance, which cites Sorensen 2013 'Metaheuristics: the metaphor exposed'.
- Targeted sampling beats grid and random because it learns from history. The hypergeometric bound P(Z=1)=K/S shows that evaluating 10^5 of 10^6 candidates still gives only a 10% chance of hitting the optimum; grid and random search are Markov-blind, metaheuristics accumulate information to focus the search.
- Selective pressure vs population diversity is the master dial (Whitley 1989). High pressure converges fast on single-modal surfaces but gets trapped on multimodal ones; low pressure searches thoroughly but wastes evaluations where there is no local-optimum risk. Every selection operator (proportionate, ranking with U in [1/M, 2/M], tournament Y, Boltzmann) is a setting of this dial.
- Constraints are handled three ways: removal (delete infeasible, loses partial information), refinement (repair to feasible, nontrivial), and penalty functions F(X)=f(X)+/-Penalty with alpha_k, beta_k, C_k where beta_k=1 gives linear penalty growth. The penalized objective is the fitness function, and it can move the unconstrained optimum away from the true constrained one.
- Measure convergence with NFE, not wall-clock, because run time confounds language, compiler, and hardware. NFE = initial solutions + iterations x solutions-per-iteration. A reliable random-search algorithm converges to similar fitness in similar NFE across k runs; low variance of the final fitness is the reliability metric.
- No free lunch on parameters. Almost every algorithm needs hand-tuned parameters (GA: M, R, PC, PM; SA: cooling schedule lambda0/beta/alpha; PSO: omega0/omegaT), the optimal set does not transfer between problems, and the book's blanket remedy is sensitivity analysis (grid over parameter combinations, multiple runs each). SOS is the parameter-free exception; CFO is the deterministic exception.
- Two structural axes classify the field: population-based vs single-point trajectory methods (TS and SA carry one solution), and memory-based vs memory-less (memory-less algorithms are a Markov process on the current state; TS's tabu list is the explicit memory case). Nature-inspired vs non-nature-inspired is the weak third axis the book itself calls unhelpful.

## Moves

- GA (Holland/Goldberg): select parents by roulette wheel over cumulative probability Qj, or ranking (Z=0 no pressure, U=2/M max), or tournament (Y=2 typical); recombine with one/two-point/uniform crossover at rate PC; perturb with uniform or shrinking nonuniform mutation at PM; preserve the elite (De Jong 1975).
- SA (Kirkpatrick/Metropolis): accept a worse move with probability exp(-delta/lambda) (Boltzmann); generate beta trial states per temperature to reach thermal equilibrium; cool geometrically lambda_new = alpha*lambda or linearly; start lambda high to escape local traps, lower it slowly.
- PSO (Kennedy/Eberhart): v_new = omega*v + C1*Rand*(Pbest - X) + C2*Rand*(Gbest - X) with C1=C2=2 and inertia omega decaying from omega0 to omegaT; clamp v to [vmin, vmax]; update X = X + v.
- DE (Storn/Price): mutate X_mut = Xr + delta*(Xr' - Xr'') with delta in [0,2] over three distinct randomly chosen agents; binomial crossover at constant C with a forced index bj so at least one gene comes from the mutant; keep the trial only if it wins (greedy). Continuous domains only.
- ACO (Dorigo): keep a pheromone matrix Ci per decision variable; update c_new = (1-rho)*c + Q/F(X) (deposit inversely proportional to fitness under minimization, evaporation rate rho); pick each variable's value with probability proportional to pheromone^alpha times heuristic eta^beta; the double-bridge/stigmergy story motivates it.
- HS (Geem): hold a harmony memory of M rows; per variable draw from memory with probability HMCR (~0.95) else random, then nudge to an adjacent pitch with probability PAR (~0.1); one new harmony per iteration replaces the worst if better.
- SFLA (Eusuff/Lansey): sort the population into Z memeplexes of Y frogs (M = Z*Y), draw a submemeplex of Q frogs by triangular probability, leap the worst toward Mbest with step Rand*(Mbest - Mworst) clamped to [Dmin, Dmax], fall back to Pbest then to a random frog, run mu memetic evolutions, then shuffle all memeplexes.
- IWO / BBO / WCA (spatial-ecology trio): IWO gives each weed seeds linear in fitness between lambda_min and lambda_max and disperses them with Gaussian N(0, sigma_t), sigma_t shrinking via nonlinear modulation index beta (r-selection to K-selection), capping the colony at Mmax by competitive exclusion; BBO migrates SIVs from high-emigration to high-immigration habitats using the species-count curves with elitism (lambda=0 for the best) and mutation Pc; WCA classifies drops into 1 sea + R rivers + streams, flows X' = X + Rand*C*(river - X) with C near 2, and evaporates+rains when a river reaches the sea within decaying threshold delta.
- FA / GSA / CFO / BA (physics-analogy set): FA moves the dimmer firefly with attractiveness beta0*exp(-gamma*d^m), m=2, gamma in [0.1,10]; GSA and CFO both use Newton gravity (F = gamma*M1*M2/d^2), GSA deriving mass from normalized fitness with a decaying gravitational constant while CFO is deterministic, seeds probes on the principal diagonal, drives acceleration from the fitness DIFFERENCE with a unit-step to forbid negative mass, and repositions deviated probes with factor phi; BA is PSO plus a loudness/pulsation-rate (A, delta) local random walk over frequency lambda in [lambda_min, lambda_max].
- SOS and CEA (the operator-rich pair): SOS runs mutualism (update both organisms with mutual vector and benefit factors beta1,beta2 in {1,2}), commensalism (update one), and parasitism (a parasite vector replaces a random host if fitter) with zero tunable parameters; CEA fuses GA+HBMO with 4 selection operators, 23 crossover types, and 4 mutation types, and auto-adjusts each operator's 'role' (its share of the next population) by measured fitness improvement, which is implicit sensitivity analysis baked into the run.
- PS (Hooke-Jeeves direct search): gradient-free; exploratory move probes 2N mesh points via GPS or N+1 via MADS around the base point, then a pattern move X_new = X + alpha*(X - X'); reset the mesh on success, contract mu by delta on failure, terminate when mu is small enough.

## Watch out

- Heavy water-resources bias. The authors are hydrologists (Bozorg-Haddad, Solgi, Loaiciga); nearly every literature review and application is reservoir operation, Muskingum flood routing, or water distribution networks. The mechanical (gear train), structural (two-bar truss), and electrical (DOCR relay coordination) examples are thin. Non-water domains need translation.
- No code, no benchmarks, no test-function results. It is pseudocode and equations only. You will not find CEC/BBOB comparison tables, convergence plots, or a repository. Pair it with an implementation library (pymoo, DEAP, scipy.optimize, nevergrad) to actually run anything.
- The one-metaphor-per-chapter format is precisely what Sorensen 2013 (cited inside the book) skewers. Many of the 20 are near-duplicates: GSA and CFO are both Newton gravity, BA is PSO plus a local walk, HBMO and CEA are GA repackaged with bee/operator dressing. Do not mistake 20 names for 20 distinct ideas.
- Equations are frequently mangled in this text extraction: dropped subscripts/superscripts, missing formula bodies left as bare '(x.y)' placeholders, and vector/matrix notation flattened. Verify every update rule against the original cited paper before you code it.
- No theory. There are no convergence proofs, no analysis of when or why a given algorithm works, no landscape characterization beyond single-modal vs multimodal. No-free-lunch appears only as a parameter-transfer caveat. It is a recipe book, not an analysis; go to book 64 for BBO's Markov-chain treatment.
- Dated to 2018 and narrow on modern practice: no CMA-ES, only passing MOPSO for multi-objective (no NSGA-II/III depth), no surrogate-assisted or Bayesian optimization, no CMA/quality-diversity, no constraint-handling beyond static penalties.
