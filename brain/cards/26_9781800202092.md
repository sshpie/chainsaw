**ISBN** 9781800202092  |  **Lobe** defensive

> The hands-on ICS/OT field manual: tool-by-tool playbook for monitoring, threat-hunting, assessing, red-teaming, and incident-responding an industrial network built on the Purdue model, with a fully reproducible Company X/Y/Z lab so every move is replayable against a SAFE testbed instead of live plant.

## Reach for this when

- The target is OT/ICS and I need to decide attacker priorities by the INVERTED CIA triad (Availability greater than Integrity greater than Confidentiality), where a SYN-flood DoS on underpowered gear is 'by far the biggest risk' and aggressive scanning itself is the threat.
- I need to place a host/protocol on the Purdue/ISA-95 skeleton (Levels 0-5 plus IDMZ at 3.5) to reason about what segment it lives in, what brokers its traffic, and what choke point it should never have crossed.
- I am modeling an ICS intrusion and need the two-phase ICS Cyber Kill Chain (Phase 1 enterprise espionage/foothold; Phase 2 Planning/Validation/ICS-attack) to structure a pentest or to attribute an observed campaign.
- I need OT-protocol probing specifics: Nmap enip-info on 44818/2222 for Rockwell, PLCSCAN on TCP 102 S7comm and 502 Modbus, Modbus-cli coil/register read-write to prove an open device has no auth.
- I am doing internet-exposure self-assessment of an industrial range with Shodan (net:CIDR narrowed by vendor strings like 'enbt OR en2t', '1761-NET-ENI/D') and want the 'on Shodan equals already compromised' rule and the vendor-fingerprint dorks.
- I am building a passive monitoring stack for a fragile network where I cannot touch endpoints: Security Onion 2, Zeek, Suricata/Snort, Wazuh, ELK, fed from SPAN/MIRROR or ESXi promiscuous vSwitch.
- I need a concrete threat-hunting hypothesis and detection workflow (beaconing, unwanted apps, suspicious external connections) including the RITA show-beacons score/interval read.
- I need a documented OT incident-response program (SANS six-step, four severity tiers, severity-tied comms windows, jump-bag spec) rather than improvising DFIR on a plant.

## Principles

- The CIA triad is INVERTED for OT: Availability first, then Integrity, then Confidentiality. Every defensive and offensive decision flips accordingly; uptime is the crown jewel, so denial and disruption outrank data theft.
- Visibility and validation beat prevention. You cannot patch decades-old open-by-design gear, so you continuously SEE (passive monitoring), VERIFY (active interrogation, threat hunting, assessments), and PROVE exploitability (red-team/pentest) against an approximation, not the live plant.
- Three monitoring postures must coexist: PASSIVE (architecture-based, never touches devices), ACTIVE (interrogating/scanning, risky on fragile OT), THREAT HUNTING (assume-compromise and go looking). Knowing which posture you are in is a safety decision.
- Threat INFORMATION is not threat INTELLIGENCE: raw IOCs become intelligence only after context, relevance, and analysis. Operationalized via OTX, MISP, honeypots (Conpot/T-Pot), GreyNoise, VirusTotal, Censys/Shodan and glue (combine, ElasticIntel, zeek-otx).
- The engineering WORKSTATION beats the PLC as a target: compromising the controls-programming host (Step 7, RSLogix, FactoryTalk) is easier and more valuable than attacking controllers directly. This is the Stuxnet pattern.
- Defender asymmetry governs the budget: 'we need to be right all the time while an attacker only needs to be right once,' and average detection takes 200-plus days. That gap is the entire justification for continuous monitoring and hunting.

## Moves

- Beaconing hunt: in Zeek conn logs filter protocol TCP, filter OUT local destination IPs, then filter payload to the ~10-10,000 byte band to surface regular-interval C2; confirm with RITA show-beacons reading score (0.996), connection count (594), avg bytes (862), and interval (600s / 10 min). Lab cut 31,416 connections to 15,276 (TCP) to 86 candidate beacons.
- OT protocol interrogation: Nmap enip-info on TCP 44818/2222 (Rockwell device/firmware), PLCSCAN on TCP 102 (S7comm) and 502 (Modbus), Modbus-cli 'modbus write 172.25.200.21 1 0' to flip a coil and read it back as one-line proof of no authentication, Grassmarlin to graph the network from a Wireshark PCAP.
- Shodan exposure self-assessment: resolve your ASN/range on ipinfo.io, run net:CIDR narrowed by vendor strings ('rockwell', 'enbt OR en2t', '1761-NET-ENI/D'); the author finds an exposed 1761-NET-ENI/D at 142.46.240.78. Rule: anything that shows up is already compromised.
- Memory forensics on a .vmem in REMnux/Volatility: imageinfo, pslist/pstree, sockets/connections, dlllist/dlldump/svcscan, yarascan to expose a malicious DLL from c:\windows\temp\ hiding inside svchost.exe as a fake service (iprip.dll, INA+ service, PID 1040, MD5 ebcf387056a5ae4e1c62f5b595b65250).
- OT credential attack chain: Kerbrute password spray (Kerberos pre-auth, near-stealth, found five accounts sharing Password123 in 0.022s), Hydra RDP brute (reports_user/Password1), dump ntds.dit via ntdsutil, impacket-secretsdump, crack with John/Ophcrack, then evil-winrm + proxychains multi-hop RDP to a ControlFlash firmware upload on Test_Left PLC 172.25.200.11.
- Build the SAFE testbed before any active OT work: ESXi + Cisco 3750 (VLAN/ACL/SPAN) + pfSense IT and OT firewalls, p2v/clone production hosts with EXACT firmware on spare PLCs, emulate stacks with python-snap7 (Siemens), pymodbus (Modbus), cpppo (CIP), python-opcua (OPC-UA), spoofed vendor MACs, simulated Chinese attacker on 222.222.222.222, iprip.dll installed as a service.
- Firmware/hardware reversing: binwalk -e a 1756-L75 .dmk, identify VxWorks/ARM, load into IDA Pro; for hardware, JTAGulator/JTAG Finder/JTAGenum to find TAP pins then XJAnalyser for extraction.
- Log-tuning for detection: enable Sysmon and PowerShell Script Block Logging (event 4104) to decode powershell -EncodedCommand, watch failed-login 4625/4771/539 and user-creation 4720/4738/4732/4728/4722.

## Watch out

- Chapter 15 (formal ICS risk-assessment mechanics) is NOT in the book body - it lives in an external GitHub PDF (Chapter_15_Industrial_Control_System_Risk_Assessments.pdf). The assessment-methodology unit is incomplete without pulling it; do not assume the Risk = f(Severity, Criticality, Likelihood, Impact) mechanics are spelled out in the prose.
- This is a Rockwell/Siemens-flavored, Windows-AD-heavy, vendor-specific lab. Tooling like enip-info, ControlFlash, RSLogix, FactoryTalk, and the Cisco-3750 testbed bias the methods toward that stack; do not over-fit to vendors or protocols not present (e.g., DNP3, BACnet, IEC 61850 get little).
- The whole book's safety thesis is DO NOT scan/fuzz/exploit live production OT. A reader skimming for offensive moves can lift the red-team and Modbus-write techniques and forget they are scoped to an approximation testbed. The active techniques are dangerous against fragile gear; running them on a real plant inverts the book's entire point.
- The lab numbers (1,000,000 events/24h, 0.996 RITA score, 600s interval, specific MD5/IPs) are illustrative of THIS testbed, not universal thresholds. Treat the beaconing byte-band (10-10,000) and score cutoffs as starting heuristics to tune, not detection constants.
- 2nd-edition currency: WannaCry/MS17-010 and Conficker/MS08-067 framing, Windows XP HMIs, and some tool versions (Security Onion 2, PoshC2, RITA flags) drift; verify current command syntax and CVE relevance before quoting in a live engagement.
