# 10. The Next Leadership Team

**ISBN** 9781000876239  |  **Lobe** human

> A 3x3x3 contingency framework for composing and running top teams (and by direct analogy, multi-agent orchestrations): pick competition vs collaboration vs balance, match it to system type and situational mandate, then keep it consistent or it rots into a known failure twin.

## Reach for this when

- I am designing an orchestrator-plus-subagent topology and need a principled call on independent lanes vs forced collaboration vs a mixed roster, not a gut pick.
- A standing process (assessment chain, disclosure pipeline, agent fleet) has gone stale or complacent and I need a deliberate anti-ossification routine.
- I am screening a team member, contractor, collaborator, or a candidate agent role and want a behavioral-anomaly checklist for toxic/low-fit signals before committing.
- I have to decide whether a change is an incremental role addition or a full re-architecture, and people keep proposing to bolt on one new component to fix a structural problem.
- I am hardening any standing system against shocks and want a resilience template that is process-shaped, not tool-shaped.
- I am writing the spec/selection stage of a delegated workflow and need to remember the ceiling is set there, not at execution.

## Principles

- No single best model. Three approaches all succeed: Team of Stars (competition, fast independent units), Synergistic Team (collaboration, consensus, whole-system optimization), Stretch Team (mix). Consistency of implementation beats choice of style.
- Every high-performance approach has a degenerate twin it slides into when its risk is ignored: Stars -> shark tank, Synergistic -> petting zoo, Stretch -> mediocracy. The Stretch/balanced option has the HIGHEST failure rate because each element is less pronounced than in the pure forms.
- Fit is contingent on system type: diversified/low-synergy -> Stars; single tightly-integrated -> Synergistic; integrated multi-business -> Stretch. Independently, the situational mandate (start-up=entrepreneur, continuation=coach, transformation/restart=decision-maker) reshapes implementation.
- Structure follows strategy: derive each role's profile from a leader mandate (a strategic diagnosis of the role's FUTURE challenges), not from an inherited or generic profile. The ceiling is set at the selection/design stage.
- Classify change before responding: capability-architecture/business-model RETAINED -> develop or add a role; MODIFIED -> full re-evaluation. Adding a chief-digital/sustainability officer cannot fix a structural shift (Henderson and Clark).
- Trust builds in four levels (knowledge, commitment, intentions, vulnerability) but over-trust causes inertia; balance trust against challenging the status quo. Values over competence: wrong-values/high-competence people very competently steer in the wrong direction.

## Moves

- Pick the orchestration archetype on the competition-collaboration axis. Independent  assessment lanes fanning out = a Team of Stars; force them only when the work genuinely needs whole-system consensus (Synergistic). Default to parallel fan-out precisely because 'enforcing collaboration in every situation is counterproductive.'
- Run the eight toxic-member signs as a behavioral-anomaly checklist on humans, collaborators, or candidate agent roles: talks in the hall but not in the room, victim mentality, criticizes others in public, etc. Treat consistent hall-vs-room divergence as the headline tell.
- Apply the six anti-complacency practices to any standing process: develop, reorganize, rotate members, institute tenure limits (Hilti's 3-year contracts, 15-year max), maintain diversity, set a renewal-focused tone (Grove's 'only the paranoid survive').
- Use the four resilience principles to harden a pipeline against shocks: multiple time horizons (temporal ambidexterity), trained crisis-management drills, group response with no profiteering, engage all layers.
- Before greenlighting a fix, force the capability-architecture-vs-business-model classification. If structural, refuse the single-role bolt-on and demand a full re-evaluation.
- Set goals at Zuempel's high-jump height: not 1.20m (no challenge), not 2.50m (won't attempt), but 1.80-1.90m where the system has to stretch. Use intrinsic motivation over financial incentives for creative senior/agent work; let incentives signal what good looks like, not drive behavior.
- Pick the decision-making mode explicitly from the five (command, advisory, collaborative vote, collaborative compromise, collaborative consensus) instead of defaulting; this is the human analogue of choosing how an orchestrator resolves subagent disagreement.

## Watch out

- Entirely non-technical. Zero on adversarial methods, threat modeling, tooling, or anything security-specific. It is a management/process layer, never security content; do not mine it for findings.
- Evidence base is CEO/headhunter interviews over five years, not controlled study. The frameworks are practitioner heuristics dressed in 3x3x3 tidiness; the named-executive anecdotes (Wipro, Nadella, Hilti) are illustrative, survivorship-biased, and not causal proof.
- The human-team-to-agent-team analogy is a thinking aid, not an isomorphism. Agents have no ego, no career incentive, no psychological safety, and no vulnerability-level trust; do not over-literalize 'manage divas' or 'build trust' onto a subagent fleet.
- The 3x3x3 grid invites false precision. Real systems are mixes and edge cases; the book itself says the Stretch/balanced cell fails most often, which is a warning against assuming the framework resolves cleanly.
- 'Consistency beats choice of style' can be misread as a license for rigidity. Read it alongside the six anti-complacency practices and the change-classification test, or it becomes an argument against ever re-architecting.
