# 30. Penetration Tester's Open Source Toolkit

**ISBN** 9781597492133  |  **Lobe** offensive

> The phase-discipline backbone of an engagement: recon-to-forensics lifecycle plus the rules-of-engagement, verification, and lab-isolation rigor that turns ad hoc tool use into a repeatable, legal, evidence-backed assessment.

## Reach for this when

- Starting any engagement and you need a phase scaffold (Intelligence Gathering -> Footprinting -> Verification -> Vitality -> enumerate -> exploit -> escalate -> report) instead of jumping straight to a scanner; this is the offensive analog of the NuClide Discover->Fingerprint->Verify->Attribute pipeline.
- Reconnaissance where the analytical filter is REACHABLE-and-RELEVANT, and you need to keep authorization as a separate legal gate from relevance (maps directly to NuClide scope-vs-relevance discipline).
- Attacking database services as a foothold: SQL Server sa/xp_cmdshell on 1433/1434, Oracle TNS listener on 1521 with default accounts (SYS/SYSTEM/SCOTT/DBSNMP), default-credential tables as primary attack surface.
- Wireless assessment where you need the full aircrack-ng kill chain (WEP PTW, WPA-PSK EAPOL dictionary) and to know WEP/WPA-PSK are dead while WPA-RADIUS has no known attack.
- Network-device compromise via misconfiguration not CVEs: default SNMP community strings public/private, Cisco type-7 weak encryption, HSRP hijack, config exfil to TFTP.
- Building or sanitizing a lab/range and you need physical+logical isolation, labeling, DoD 5220.22-M wipe, and a malware-as-biohazard handling posture.
- Forensically sound acquisition during an engagement: read-only source mount, dd/dcfldd hashwindow, MD5/SHA1 verification, file carving with Foremost.
- You need a methodology-framework decision (OSSTMM vs NIST SP 800-42 vs ISSAF/PTF) to justify a structured engagement plan to a client or CERT.

## Principles

- Recon is a reiterative four-phase loop feeding back into itself, not a one-shot step; you re-verify and re-confirm vitality continuously, not once.
- Every level of access parlays upward: a cracked router-config password gets reused, a wireless foothold pivots to the SQL Server, an intern's bbanter/bbanter SSH login climbs to the senior sysadmin. The chain is always worse than the sum of its parts.
- Understanding WHY a tool works beats memorizing switches; open-source tools build deeper skill precisely because they are not point-and-click, giving the attacker's perspective commercial scanners hide.
- Read source before you run it: the author hand-fixed cge.pl because it only matched '200 ok' not '200 OK' - 'If you like your system security, you will never run a binary-only exploit.'
- The dominant network-device weakness is configuration error, not software bugs; misconfiguration is the highest-yield, most-overlooked surface.
- Discipline (rules of engagement, evidence handling, lab isolation, restraint) is what separates a professional assessment from reckless hacking; the Morris worm is the cautionary tale for not testing on a simulator first.

## Moves

- Recon loop: AXFR zone-transfer attempts, reverse-DNS brute with bf-dns.pl over a netenum range (forward and reverse zones are separate databases), BiLE bi-directional link analysis with TLD/domain-name expansion to find related hosts.
- Nmap scan-type selection by intent: -sS SYN, -sT connect, -sF/-sX/-sN stealth, -sA ACK firewall-map, -sI idle/zombie blind scan, -sU UDP, plus -sV version and -O OS fingerprint, with timing T0 Paranoid through T5 Insane; fingerprint passively with p0f, actively with Xprobe2, web with httprint.
- DB foothold: SQL injection ' OR 1=1-- for auth bypass, command injection 127.0.0.1 | ipconfig, squeeza's three blind channels (DNS request, DB error message, timing); Oracle priv-esc via glogin.sql by poisoning the lsnrctl log to inject 'create user attacker ...; grant dba'.
- Buffer-overflow fuzzing in Perl (sendraw) against the eEye IIS .printer overflow: locate EBP at 268 bytes, overwrite EIP at 272 with 42424242 (BBBB).
- Full WEP crack: airmon-ng monitor -> airodump-ng capture IVs -> aireplay-ng fake-auth (-1), fragmentation (-5)/chopchop (-4) for PRGA -> packetforge-ng craft ARP -> aireplay-ng ARP replay (-2/-3) -> aircrack-ng FMS/KoreK or PTW (-z); WEP recoverable 50% at 40,000 packets, 95% at 85,000, in 3-4 minutes.
- WPA-PSK: force the 4-way EAPOL handshake with aireplay-ng deauth (-0), then cowpatty -f wordlist -r dump -s SSID, or genpmk precomputed tables (-d) at ~60,000 passphrases/sec; macchanger -m to bypass MAC filters after enumerating allowed clients in Kismet.
- Router compromise: onesixtyone SNMP community brute, snmpwalk/snmpset to force running-config to a TFTP server, Hydra (-t 2) on Telnet+enable (guessed p4ssw0rd in 22s), IRPAS hsrp to hijack the HSRP virtual IP, Cisco Global Exploiter for known device bugs.
- Forensically sound imaging: boot 'bt nohd', mount -o ro, dd if=/dev/hda bs=512 conv=sync,noerror | split -b 640m (or dcfldd hashwindow=0, or pipe over netcat), verify md5sum; carve with foremost -t jpeg -i image and Magicrescue, parse IE history with pasco, thumbs.db with Vinetto, dump live RAM with memfetch.

## Watch out

- 2007 snapshot tied to BackTrack 2 (SLAX/.lzm live CD); specific versions, URLs, and the live-CD mechanics are dead. Kali replaced BackTrack. Teach the methodology and tool-thinking, not the command syntax verbatim.
- WEP is essentially extinct as a real-world target and the WEP chapter is now museum material; the value is the kill-chain reasoning, not a deliverable attack. WPA-PSK dictionary attack survives but modern WPA2/WPA3 and PMKID-based captures changed the capture step.
- The SQLi/XSS web-app surface predates modern frameworks, ORMs, prepared-statement defaults, and CSP; do not bring 2007 payloads to a 2026 web target expecting them to land. Defer to #17/#25 for current technique.
- Nessus and the commercial-vs-open-source framing reflect a 2006 market; Nessus is no longer free, and the OSSTMM/NIST SP 800-42/ISSAF framework survey is dated against current PTES, OWASP, and NIST SP 800-115. Use the decision logic, refresh the framework names.
- Oracle glogin.sql/lsnrctl log-poisoning escalation and the eEye IIS .printer overflow are version-specific and long-patched; they illustrate the technique class, not a live primitive.
- Market facts cited (70% web servers, Apache+IIS ~90%, Oracle 44.4% in 2006, 1B+ Bluetooth devices) are stale numbers; do not quote them as current.
