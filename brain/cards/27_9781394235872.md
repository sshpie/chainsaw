# 27. AI for Good

**ISBN** 9781394235872  |  **Lobe** ai-agentic

> A field-tested failure-mode taxonomy and applied-ML deployment playbook from Microsoft's AI for Good Lab - reach for it when you need a checklist of the nine ways a model deceives its operator, or a candid production-LLM-agent build log.

## Reach for this when

- Red-teaming or auditing an ML/LLM model and you want a structured pre-trust checklist: run the nine-lesson framework (bias, correlation-not-causation, algorithmic discrimination, shortcut learning, out-of-distribution, gaming/Goodhart, weaponization, illusion of certainty, can't-solve-alone) before you believe any reported metric.
- You suspect a model is winning on a leaked or shortcut feature (the 2017 dermatology-model-reads-surgical-rulers pattern); you need the vocabulary and the train/test-split discipline to prove it.
- Building or assessing a production LLM agent and want a real dual-engine routing build log (QuitBot: deterministic Azure QnA + fine-tuned GPT-3.5, gatekeeper model, automated entropy/PPL plus human eval gating) - chains directly with books 38/39/40/41.
- You are arguing about an evaluation baseline (benchmark against the average accessible operator at the 50th-to-75th percentile, not the world's top expert) and need the medicine-grounded version of that argument.
- Defending or attacking aggregated/synthetic data releases: the broadband chapter names membership-inference attacks explicitly and demonstrates differential privacy (Laplace Mechanism, epsilon 0.1/0.2, OpenDP SmartNoise) at ZIP-code aggregation as the defense.
- Scoping a client engagement where success is a mission outcome, not an F1 - you need the Communication/Data/Modeling/Impact structure and the 11 takeaways to set expectations against media hype.
- Designing an anomaly-detection-at-scale pipeline (satellite change detection finding new deforestation roads / disaster damage in minutes instead of days) and want a proven human-in-the-loop active-learning pattern for rare positives.

## Principles

- Verification and explainability are load-bearing, not optional - 'AI techniques are useful but limited: they must be explainable to be useful.' The scan/model produces candidates; verification produces the finding. This is the same  tenet, stated in ML terms.
- A model surfaces association, not mechanism. Predictive power is not causation - DAGs, confounders, and 'explanations are predictive rather than causal' (stated even for the glass-box EBM). Never let a strong correlation read as a cause.
- Goodhart's Law is a structural property of optimization, not a bug: 'when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure' (cobra effect, phone-rocker step gaming). Any metric you optimize will be gamed - design evaluation expecting it.
- Train/validation/test splits must mirror the deployment scenario or the performance estimate lies. Split ACROSS disaster types, across hospitals, across noise conditions. Same-population leakage gives 'falsely optimistic performance' (otoscopy: AUC 0.95 internal vs 0.76 external).
- Out-of-distribution collapse is the most common real-world break; a model trained on one hospital / one audio quality / one disaster type generalizes nothing. Detection-evasion and robustness testing live here.
- The 'last mile' of deployment and adoption is harder than modeling and lies mostly outside the technical team's control - open-sourcing builds trust, and impact KPIs (time saved, coverage) sit several steps removed from F1/AUC.

## Moves

- Run the nine-lesson taxonomy as a literal diagnostic checklist against any model or finding before trusting it - treat each lesson as a probe: is this a shortcut feature? is the split leaking? is the metric being gamed?
- When a reported metric looks too clean, force an out-of-distribution split: re-evaluate across the dimension that will vary in deployment (cohort, disaster type, noise, hospital) and watch for the 0.95-to-0.76 collapse that exposes shortcut learning.
- For LLM-agent work, copy the QuitBot architecture: dual-engine routing (deterministic in-knowledge-base path + generative out-of-library path via a gatekeeper/router), and gate output with automated scores (entropy for pertinence, PPL for grammaticalness) PLUS human raters on accuracy/repetitiveness/tone. The bottleneck is domain-knowledge-base depth, not model size - DialoGPT-345M lost to GPT-3.5-175B, but the real fix was 11,000 curated QnA pairs.
- Attack or defend aggregated data with the membership-inference lens: aggregated/synthetic releases are 'vulnerable to membership inference attacks'; the demonstrated mitigation is differential privacy (Laplace Mechanism, epsilon budgets, OpenDP SmartNoise) applied at the aggregation boundary.
- For rare-positive detection at scale, build a human-in-the-loop active-learning pipeline: surface only the tiles/records most likely to contain the positive (whale detection on satellite imagery), instead of brute-force labeling everything.
- Reframe an evaluation baseline argument: benchmark the system against the average accessible operator (50th-to-75th percentile), not the top expert, when the deployment population is underserved - the JAMA April 2023 GPT-4 result (more accurate AND more empathetic than physicians on patient queries) is the citation.
- Reach for the glass-box pattern when a black-box model is undeployable: an EBM (evolved GAM) gave calibrated, auditable probability vectors AND beat MOCA on pancreatic cysts (could cut unnecessary surgery 59%) - explainability bought adoption, not just trust.

## Watch out

- This is a case-study anthology, not a deep technical or adversarial text. The ~25 remote-sensing and clinical chapters are pattern-recognition material; do not mine them for implementation depth. The high-value chapters are 1-3 (failure modes), QuitBot, broadband-privacy, and the epilogue - the rest is skim.
- Microsoft-philanthropy framing. The book sells the convening thesis and the Lab's brand; read the methodology, discount the marketing (royalties-to-charity, '100+ projects' positioning).
- The LLM material is dated 2020-2023 (GPT-3 Davinci/Curie, fine-tuned GPT-3.5-175B, DialoGPT-345M). The architecture insights transfer; the specific model choices and capabilities do not - do not treat its model comparisons as current.
- 'Compare to the average accessible operator, not the top expert' is a deployment-impact argument, not a safety bar. For high-stakes or adversarial contexts a beats-the-median baseline is the wrong threshold - do not import it into a security-assurance claim where the worst case governs.
- Reported headline metrics (Jaccard 0.63, recall 87%, AUC 96.5%, 92.5%) are partner-deployment numbers under specific splits, not transferable benchmarks. The book's own lesson applies to itself: these numbers will not generalize to your distribution.
