# 58. Biological Computation

**ISBN** 9781420087956  |  **Lobe** bio-compute

> Lamm and Unger map the four biology-inspired computing paradigms (cellular automata, evolutionary computation, neural networks, molecular DNA computation) plus swarm intelligence, artificial immune systems, artificial life, and systems biology, each reduced to its named algorithm, governing equation, and honest failure mode.

## Reach for this when

- Reasoning about an evolutionary or coverage-guided fuzzer and needing the schema theorem, building-block hypothesis, and the crossover/mutation/selection operator taxonomy that sits under genetic search.
- Modeling an anomaly-based network intrusion detector on self/nonself lines: LISYS negative selection, r-contiguous-bit matching, tolerization and activation thresholds.
- Sanity-checking a claimed DNA or molecular-computation result: what Adleman, Lipton, Winfree, and Shapiro actually demonstrated versus the exponential molecule-count ceiling.
- Grounding a swarm or metaheuristic optimizer (ACO, PSO) before tuning it: the transition-probability rule, pheromone evaporation, inertia omega and c1/c2.
- Explaining computational universality and emergence from simple local rules (Game of Life, Rule 110) to a skeptical audience.
- Building a resilience or chaos-engineering analogy from biological robustness: Kitano's four mechanisms and the yeast gene-knockout data.
- Choosing a classical neural model for a small task and needing the honest limits: perceptron linear separability, Hopfield capacity, overfitting.

## Principles

- Universality from simple local rules: Conway's Game of Life (birth on exactly 3 neighbors, survival on 2 or 3) is Turing-complete via glider logic gates, and Wolfram's Rule 110 is universal, while across the 256 elementary CA rules roughly 85% are trivial, 10% complex, 5% chaotic. Simple plus local plus parallel equals arbitrary computation.
- Holland's schema theorem: short, low-order, above-average-fitness schemata (building blocks) get exponentially increasing trials, where a schema H has order o(H) and defining length d(H), and implicit parallelism means one population of n strings usefully samples on the order of n^3 schemata.
- Molecular parallelism is real but bounded: Adleman's 1994 DNA Hamiltonian-path solution runs in a number of lab steps linear in graph size, but the molecule count grows as d^N, so an 80-vertex instance would want about 10^80 molecules and the parallelism never beats the exponential blowup.
- Generate-and-test is SIMD at Avogadro scale: DNA algorithms have two phases, random parallel construction of every candidate (base-pairing plus ligase) then a linear sequence of extraction operations applied simultaneously to all molecules, which is single-instruction-multiple-data.
- Self/nonself discrimination by negative selection: randomly generated detectors that match self during a tolerization window are deleted and survivors flag anomalies. LISYS encodes each connection as a 49-bit IP/IP/port tuple, matches on r-contiguous bits (r=12), fires at activation threshold tau=10, and memory detectors drop tau to 1.
- Classical neural models have provable limits: a single perceptron separates only linearly separable sets (Minsky and Papert, XOR fails), a Hopfield net stores about 0.138N to 0.15N patterns before spurious attractors dominate, and backprop rides the sigmoid derivative y(1-y) and overfits without generalization control.
- Robustness is engineered by four mechanisms (Kitano): negative-feedback control, modularity as near-decomposable systems (Simon's Hora and Tempus watchmakers), redundancy, and structural stability; empirically 82% of yeast's roughly 6000 genes are individually dispensable and only 15% affect growth rate.
- Stigmergy is coordination through the environment, not direct messaging: ants deposit evaporating pheromones, and ACO's random-proportional rule weights each edge by tau^alpha times eta^beta over the neighbors, the same indirect-signaling pattern that drives ant-colony routing of network packets.

## Moves

- Adleman DNA Hamiltonian path: encode each vertex as a random 20-base oligo, each edge as (last 10 bases of source) plus (first 10 bases of target), anneal with ligase to build all paths in parallel, amplify by PCR with start/end primers, select correct length on a gel (path length n times fixed vertex length), then extract vertex-by-vertex with magnetic beads on complementary probes.
- Lipton SAT-by-DNA: build the 2^n-assignment graph (upper edge = 1, lower edge = 0 per variable), generate all assignments with ligase, then for each clause C_i select and keep only sequences that satisfy it into tube T_i; a nonempty T_m means satisfiable. Operation count is linear in literals l, and linear in clauses m for fixed clause width.
- Shapiro/Benenson enzymatic finite automaton: FokI recognizes GGATG and cleaves 9/13 bases downstream leaving a 4-base sticky end; encode each symbol in 6 bases, encode state by displacement Delta (q1 uses the first 4 bases, q0 the last 4), and set the next state by spacer length via Delta_new = 3 + (Delta_current - spacer), with spacer lengths 1/3/5 realizing the eight two-state transition rules; read out the final state by gel-sized output detectors.
- Winfree DNA tiling: implement Wang tiles as four-armed DNA junctions (Winfree et al. 1998); a seed tile S plus two frame tiles plus four compute tiles self-assemble into a binary counter (Barish, Rothemund, Winfree 2005). Universal in theory, but low self-assembly yield and high mismatch rate in practice.
- Genetic algorithm core loop: fixed-length binary chromosomes, fitness-proportional (roulette), rank, tournament, steady-state, or elitism selection, single- or two-point crossover at rate pc, bit-flip mutation at rate pm, plus inversion; guard against premature convergence with fitness scaling (linear f' = a*f + b, or Cmax windowing).
- Genetic programming (Koza): evolve Lisp S-expression trees over a terminal set T and function set F, initialize with full, grow, or ramped-half-and-half, mutate by node replacement, and score with a task metric such as the Matthews correlation coefficient for a classifier.
- Ant Colony Optimization for TSP: place ants per city, move by p_ij proportional to tau_ij^alpha times eta_ij^beta (heuristic eta = 1/d), deposit pheromone inversely to tour length, evaporate a fixed fraction each step, and optionally go elitist (only the best ant deposits) with min/max pheromone caps.
- Particle Swarm Optimization: each particle keeps position x, velocity v, personalbest, and globalbest; update v = omega*v + c1*rand()*(globalbest - x) + c2*rand()*(personalbest - x) then x += v, with omega near 1 and c1 = c2 = 2 initially and a fresh rand() per dimension. Trained a 5-input, 3-hidden, 1-output battery-charge net in 2.2 minutes versus 3.5 hours for backprop.
- LISYS network intrusion detection: 49-bit self-strings, randomly generated detectors, delete any that match self within tolerization T = 4 days, activate on tau = 10 matches at r = 12 contiguous bits, require human co-stimulation within Ts = 1 day, and clone memory detectors to neighbor nodes (tau drops to 1); 100 detectors/node caught 7 of 7 intrusions at 1.76 false positives/day.
- Avida digital-organism experiment: seed a self-replicating genome using h-alloc, h-search, h-copy, h-divide with nop-A/nop-B templates, set a per-instruction copy-error (mutation) rate, and reward computed logic tasks (EQU highest) with extra CPU time; reproduces Tierra's shrinking-genome and parasite arms-race dynamics (Ray 1992, Lenski et al. 2003).

## Watch out

- This is a 2011 undergraduate teaching survey, not a reference implementation. It gives the concepts and the math (schema theorem, perceptron convergence, Hopfield energy) with pseudo-code and toy examples, not production algorithms; go to books 62, 64, and 65 for engineering-grade metaheuristics.
- Molecular computation is presented honestly as not competitive with digital computers: the authors name two obstacles (per-problem algorithm-design cost and physical error rate) and the molecule count is exponential (d^N). Do not let it seed a DNA-breaks-crypto overstatement.
- The security-relevant content is thin and dated: LISYS (Hofmeyr and Forrest 2000) is the single intrusion-detection case, and its parameters are tuned to a 20-day 1990s LAN capture. Treat negative selection as a mental model for anomaly detection, not a deployable IDS.
- No modern deep learning, transformers, or GPU training; the neural chapter stops at Hopfield and self-organizing maps. Pair with books 24 and 61 for anything current.
- Bioinformatics (sequence alignment, pattern matching on DNA/RNA/protein) is explicitly out of scope by the authors' own statement; if the task is sequence analysis this is the wrong book, use 55.
- Key results are cited at survey depth from primary papers (Adleman Science 1994, Winfree Nature 1998, Benenson Nature 2001/2004, Lenski Nature 2003); verify the original before quoting exact experimental parameters.
