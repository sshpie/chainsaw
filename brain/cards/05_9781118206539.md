# 05. The Art of Coaching

**ISBN** 9781118206539  |  **Lobe** human

> A disciplined practice for changing how a person behaves, what they believe, and how they show up - reach for it when you need to move another human (or yourself) toward a hard change without telling them what to do.

## Reach for this when

- Mentoring a junior researcher or running a subagent-orchestration debrief where the goal is durable capability, not a one-off fix - Block's standard 'measure your work by the optimism and self-sufficiency you leave behind' is the orchestration ideal applied to people.
- A disclosure conversation where the recipient is defensive, scared, or anchored, and a CVE dump will trigger fight-or-flight - you need the Facilitative stances (cathartic/catalytic/supportive) to lower anxiety before any technical content lands.
- You catch yourself or a teammate locked in a 'rut story' (victim, tranquilizing, 'why bother') about a target, a tool failure, or a stalled engagement, and you need to reframe into a 'river story' that restores agency.
- Self-management before a high-stakes irreversible action (push/publish/deploy) - the 'box' visualization and 5-minute grounding ritual is a pre-flight check against tunnel vision and confirmation bias.
- You suspect your own reasoning is climbing the Ladder of Inference too fast - asserting a finding actionable from selected data plus assumptions rather than observable 200-with-data - and need to walk back down to the rung that is actually verified.
- Designing a learning or onboarding sequence (a skill, a methodology handoff, a training cohort) and need the adult-learning spine: Zone of Proximal Development, scaffolding, gradual release of responsibility.

## Principles

- Work three domains at once - behaviors, beliefs, and being (ontology). Changing only behavior is shallow and does not stick; the durable lever is the belief and the way of being underneath the action.
- The Ladder of Inference is a debugging tool for cognition: observable data -> selected data -> meanings -> assumptions -> conclusions -> beliefs -> actions. Most bad calls are made high on the ladder; verification means walking back down to observable data. This is the cognitive twin of the  'verification is the load-bearing stage' rule.
- Choose your stance deliberately, do not default to the comfortable one. Heron's six: Facilitative (cathartic, catalytic, supportive) and Directive (confrontational, informative, prescriptive). Aim Facilitative ~75-80% of the time; name and vary the mode consciously.
- Adults cannot learn when anxiety is high or the work sits outside their Zone of Proximal Development. Anxiety is a learning-blocker first and a personality trait never. Scaffold inside the ZPD, then release responsibility gradually.
- Hold two truths: stay unattached to the other person's specific choices, stay firmly attached to the outcome that matters. Autonomy and a non-negotiable purpose coexist - you coach the person, not the answer.
- The coach's own being is the instrument. A grounded, present, non-judgmental state is itself a move - 'Who you are speaks so loud that I cannot hear what you are saying.' Self-management precedes technique.

## Moves

- Walk the Ladder of Inference: when someone (or you) asserts a conclusion, ask 'what observable data did you actually see?' and rebuild upward, surfacing the selected data and assumptions inserted along the way. Use it to refute your own framing before it hardens into a 'finding.'
- Pick a stance and name it before you speak: cathartic (release the emotion), catalytic (draw out their own thinking), supportive (affirm), confrontational (challenge a contradiction), informative (give knowledge), prescriptive (tell them what to do). Default Facilitative; go Directive only when the ZPD requires it.
- Run the Arc of a Coaching Conversation as a session template: check in -> 'What's on your mind?' -> 'How'd that go?' -> engage stances -> next steps -> reflect/feedback. Talk less than roughly one-quarter of the total time.
- Get grounded before high-stakes work: 5 minutes of breathing, then the 'box' visualization - stuff the judgmental/anchoring thoughts in a box, lid on, reclaim them later. A pre-flight ritual against tunnel vision before any irreversible action.
- Diagnose with the six lenses (Coach's Optical Refractor): Inquiry, Change Management, Systems Thinking, Adult Learning, Systemic Oppression, Emotional Intelligence - cycle through them on a stuck problem to break single-lens fixation.
- Build a SMARTE work plan for any capability-building engagement: Strategic/Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Results-based, Time-bound, plus Equitable. The plan is the spine, equivalent to a long-term unit plan, not a checklist.
- Apply the 5:1 magic ratio when giving feedback - five positive/affirming interactions per corrective one. The Marcus transcript showed ~97% of one teacher's interactions were corrective; the inverse is what builds trust and learning.
- Reframe rut stories into river stories: when the narrative is constricting and defensive ('why bother', 'they always', 'I can't'), name it as a rut story and co-construct an empowering river story that restores agency, then run Five Whys to root cause.

## Watch out

- Domain-specific to 2013-era US urban K-12 schools (Common Core, NCLB, Oakland Unified). The equity-in-education framing, suspension data, and National Equity Project lineage are load-bearing context, not transferable verbatim - extract the mechanics (ladder, stances, ZPD, river/rut), not the school-reform politics.
- Single-author, narrative and case-study evidence base. The Ana 72%-suspension-cut and Marcus 97%-corrective stories are illustrative anecdotes, not controlled studies. Do not cite them as proof; treat them as worked examples. For empirical coaching-effectiveness data, go to the studies in the book's own Appendix E.
- 'Transformational' coaching reaches into beliefs and being - that is invasive by design. In a professional/adversarial security context this can read as manipulation; the autonomy-preserving 'two truths' discipline is what keeps it ethical, and book 19 (Social Engineering) shows how the same toolkit weaponizes when that discipline is dropped.
- The equity 'E' in SMARTE and the Lens of Systemic Oppression are tied to a specific social-justice purpose; do not hollow them into generic corporate goal-setting and pretend you kept the book's thesis - that strips the non-negotiable purpose that makes the method coherent.
- Heavily relational and slow by intent. It is the wrong tool for mondo-mode terse execution or a one-shot directive task; reach for it only when the work is genuinely about moving or growing a human over time.
