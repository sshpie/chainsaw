# 63. Computational and Visualization Techniques for Structural Bioinformatics Using Chimera

**ISBN** 9781439836613  |  **Lobe** bio-compute

> Burkowski's algorithmic tour of UCSF Chimera: how to script a molecular viewer in Python to compute structure (spatial hashing, inertial axes, dihedrals, Kabsch superposition, dynamic-programming alignment, Lennard-Jones energy, dead-end elimination, residue networks) and render the answer as a scenographic surface, all grounded in 140+ downloadable StructBio scripts.

## Reach for this when

- You need to script a molecular viewer instead of clicking it: driving UCSF Chimera from Python (import chimera, openModels.open('1AY7', type='PDB')) to fetch a PDB, walk the Molecule > Residue > Atom > Bond hierarchy, and read atom.coord(), atom.bfactor, residue.phi/psi, residue.kdHydrophobicity as first-class attributes.
- You are doing spatial neighbor queries on a point cloud (atoms, particles, anything embedded in 3D) and want an O(n)-build O(1)-query grid/cell-list instead of an O(n^2) all-pairs distance loop: the Ch3 Shell class binning into cubes of side maxR with a 27-cube (3x3x3) inspection.
- You must superpose two structures or point sets and report a real RMSD: the Kabsch/SVD rotation matrix (C = sum y_i x_i^T, SVD C = U S V^T, R = U V^T, det-fix for reflection) with the 0/1-3/>3 Angstrom similarity bands.
- You need to align two sequences or two structures by dynamic programming: Needleman-Wunsch global and Smith-Waterman local with BLOSUM62 plus a gap penalty, and the structural extensions (SSE-quadrilateral, QV quality value, DALI/SSAP/VAST/CE families).
- You are scoring or minimizing a molecular conformation: the potential-energy decomposition E = Ebond + Eangle + Edihedral + Evdw + Ecoulomb, the Lennard-Jones 12-6 and Coulomb terms with the O(n^2) double sum and ~9-10 Angstrom cutoff, force fields AMBER/CHARMM/GROMOS/OPLS, and driving an Amber zero-step minimization for a single energy read.
- You are packing side chains or doing any NP-hard discrete combinatorial placement: backbone-dependent Dunbrack rotamer libraries, chi1-chi5 dihedrals, and Goldstein dead-end elimination (DEE) as the pruning that makes 60^65 exhaustive search tractable (SCWRL4 lineage).
- You want to reason about a protein as a graph: residue interaction networks (nodes = residues, edges = contacts under a threshold), allostery and contact-rearrangement networks with a rearrangement factor RF(i,j), small-world structure, and the Appendix-C graph toolkit (Dijkstra shortest path, DFS connected components, Delaunay tessellation).
- You need to generate publication-grade 3D geometry programmatically: the scenographic ParametricSurfaces (Frenet-frame ribbons, extrusion, revolution, ruled surfaces), LeastSquaresPlane, InertialAxes, and matplotlib contact/distance/Ramachandran maps.

## Principles

- The molecular viewer is a programmable object model, not a GUI. Chimera exposes a strict hierarchy (Molecule > Residue > Atom > Bond) plus a Sequence class, and every geometric or chemical fact is an attribute you read in Python: atom.coord() returns a Point, atom.idatmType comes from the IDATM typer, atom.radius/bfactor are live, residue.isHelix/isSheet/isStrand/ssId/phi/psi/chi1-4 and kdHydrophobicity are precomputed. Scripting beats clicking because the answer is a value, not a picture.
- There are three different residue numbering indices and confusing them corrupts every result: the 3D-structure index, the resMap sequence index, and id.position (1-based). NMR ensembles compound this (1ZNF holds 37 models, 1AY7 is a complex), so any script must be explicit about which model and which index it walks.
- Spatial hashing turns O(n^2) into O(n). The Shell class lays a bounding box over the molecule in cubes of side maxR, keys a dict by integer (i,j,k) grid coordinates via floor((coord - minVals)/maxR), and answers any within-maxR neighbor query by inspecting only the 27 cubes (3x3x3) around the query cube. Every contact map, dehydron scan, and van der Waals term rides on this one idea.
- Geometry is linear algebra you can name. A bond angle is arccos of a normalized dot product; a dihedral (phi/psi/omega) is the signed angle between two plane normals built from cross products; inertial axes are the eigenvectors of the inertial tensor (smallest eigenvalue = long axis, a Lagrange-multiplier problem); a best-fit plane is a least-squares/SVD fit. Ramachandran validation (PROCHECK, a 36x36 = 1296-cell grid of 10-degree bins over 121,870 residues from 463 structures) is just phi/psi binned.
- Structural superposition is a solved closed-form problem: Kabsch via SVD. Center both point sets, form the covariance C = sum y_i x_i^T, take SVD C = U S V^T, set R = U V^T, and correct a reflection when det(R) < 0 by flipping the sign of the third column (R = U diag(1,1,-1) V^T). RMSD then reads directly: 0 identical, 1-3 Angstrom similar, >3 Angstrom low similarity.
- Sequence and structure alignment are the same dynamic-programming skeleton (Bellman 1954). Needleman-Wunsch fills a global score matrix with BLOSUM62 substitution scores minus a gap penalty and traces from the corner; Smith-Waterman floors every cell at 0 and traces back from the maximum cell for local alignment. Structural alignment swaps the scoring for SSE-quadrilateral geometry or a QV quality value (R0 = 3 Angstrom).
- Molecular energy is a sum of physically-named terms over an O(n^2) pair loop. E_total = Ebond + Eangle + Edihedral + Evdw + Ecoulomb, with van der Waals as Lennard-Jones 12-6 (A/r^12 - B/r^6) and electrostatics as Coulomb (Kc*qi*qj/(eps*r), Kc = 332). The nonbonded double sum is the cost center, tamed by a ~9-10 Angstrom cutoff and sigmoidal distance-dependent dielectric; force fields (AMBER Cornell et al, CHARMM, GROMOS, OPLS, MMFF) differ only in parameters and functional form.
- Discrete conformational search is combinatorial and needs pruning, not brute force. Side-chain packing is NP-hard: 1CRN has 65 rotatable side chains and 60^65 exhaustive placements is hopeless. Backbone-dependent Dunbrack rotamer libraries discretize the continuum, and Goldstein dead-end elimination removes any rotamer whose best-case self+pair energy still loses to an alternative's worst case, collapsing the search (the SCWRL4 approach). This is a general lesson for any NP-hard placement, not just proteins.

## Moves

- Fetch and walk a structure: chimera.openModels.open('1AY7', type='PDB') then iterate mol.residues and res.atoms; read atom.coord(), atom.bfactor, atom.idatmType, atom.radius, res.phi, res.psi, res.kdHydrophobicity, res.isHelix/isStrand, res.id.position/chainId/ssId. For NMR ensembles iterate the model list (1ZNF = 37 models) explicitly.
- Cell-list neighbor search (Ch3 Shell): __convCoordsToGridCoords does floor((coords - minVals)/maxR) to a 3-tuple key; gridDict maps (i,j,k) -> atom list; getAtomsInShell loops range(xI-1, xI+2) x range(yI-1, yI+2) x range(zI-1, zI+2) and distance-filters only those 27 buckets. Build once, query O(1). Reuse verbatim for any 3D proximity problem.
- Contact and distance maps: build a residue-residue distance matrix from the Shell neighbors and render with matplotlib pcolor; threshold it for a binary contact map that feeds the Ch8 residue network.
- Dehydron detection: place a desolvation sphere of radius 6.5 Angstrom at each alpha carbon of a backbone H-bond, count carbonaceous CHn atoms inside, and flag the H-bond as a dehydron (under-wrapped, sticky) when the count is <= 19. Uses the HBondDicts categories HE/PL/AP/NA.
- Inertial axes and best-fit plane: form the inertial tensor, solve the eigenvalue problem, take the eigenvector of the smallest eigenvalue as the principal axis (InertialAxes / InertialAxisAlignment classes); LeastSquaresPlane for a fitted plane through a residue patch.
- Kabsch superposition: u, s, vt = numpy.linalg.svd(c_A); rotMatrix = dot(u, vt); if linalg.det(rotMatrix) < 0: rotMatrix = dot(u * array([1.0, 1.0, -1.0]), vt). Apply to the mobile set, then compute RMSD. Overlapper/InertialAxisAlignment wrap the workflow.
- Smith-Waterman local alignment: sw[i,j] = max(sw[i-1,j-1] + blosum62(a_i, b_j), sw[i-1,j] - penalty, sw[i,j-1] - penalty, 0); track the argmax cell and traceback to the first 0. Swap the 0-floor and corner-start for Needleman-Wunsch global. SequencesLocalAlignment class packages it.
- Single energy evaluation via Amber: add hydrogens, assign charges (Antechamber for ligands), and run a zero-step minimization base.Minimizer(nsteps=0, cgsteps=0, addhyd=True) through MMTK so the force field returns E_total without moving an atom. LJ_evaluation class for a standalone Lennard-Jones term; a linear capped repulsive pseudo-energy (Canutescu/Dunbrack, capped ~10 kcal/mol) as a cheap clash proxy.
- Rotamer placement and DEE: getRotamers(residue, ...) pulls the Dunbrack backbone-dependent library, useRotamer applies a choice; drive Goldstein dead-end elimination by comparing self-energy + summed pair-energy bounds to prune dominated rotamers before any full search. chi1-chi4 exposed by Chimera (arginine actually has chi1-chi5).
- Residue network + graph analysis: build nodes = residues, edges = contacts under a distance threshold, then use the Appendix-C GraphBase/Graph toolkit: getShortestPath (Dijkstra-like), computeConnectedComponents (DFS), induced subgraphs, and Delaunay tessellation for spatial adjacency. Compute a rearrangement factor RF(i,j) across two states (phosphofructokinase 6PFK vs 4PFK) to map allosteric contact-rearrangement networks.
- Scenographic rendering: ParametricSurfaces subclasses (FrenetFrameSurface = base curve swept along a spine with tangent-aligned rotation matrices, ExtrusionSurface, RevolutionSurface, RuledSurface) plus Surfaces/Solids/Lines/LabelGroups to emit custom ribbons, tubes, and annotated geometry; pickle the surface bundle (Appendix D) to reload without recompute.

## Watch out

- This is Python 2 against a specific UCSF Chimera API (import chimera, raw_input, the openModels/Molecule object model). Chimera has been superseded by ChimeraX with a different Python 3 API, so the scripts are load-bearing as algorithms and patterns but will not run verbatim on current builds. Read for the method, port the calls.
- The value is the ~140 downloadable StructBio scripts and their algorithms, not novel science. Burkowski wrote this as a companion to his own Structural Bioinformatics: An Algorithmic Approach; several chapters assume you also have that text for the derivations. It is a how-to-implement book, not a first-principles reference.
- The three residue-numbering indices (3D-structure index vs resMap sequence index vs id.position) are a persistent foot-gun; an off-by-one or a wrong-model read silently corrupts contact maps, alignments, and energies. Nothing warns you. Assert the index and model explicitly in every script.
- The energy code is pedagogical and O(n^2). The Lennard-Jones/Coulomb double sum with a 9-10 Angstrom cutoff and sigmoidal dielectric is fine for a single-structure read but is not a production MD engine; for real simulation use AMBER/CHARMM/OpenMM/GROMACS, and for the energy read the book itself defers to MMTK/Amber via a zero-step minimizer.
- Nothing here is security. The only offensive-security transfer is by analogy: the graph reachability toolkit maps to attack graphs, the DP alignment maps to diffing, and the PDB/MOL2 parsers plus urllib2 fetch are an attack surface to note, not an exploit the book provides. Those bridges are ours to build through the chained books.
- Force-field numbers and parameter sets (Kc = 332, Cornell et al AMBER charges, Dunbrack library versions) are era-specific and force-field-specific; results are only comparable within one consistent force field, and cross-force-field energy comparisons are meaningless. Fix the force field before you trust an absolute energy.
