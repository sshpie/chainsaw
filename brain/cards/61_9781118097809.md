# 61. Computational Intelligence and Pattern Analysis in Biological Informatics

**ISBN** 9781118097809  |  **Lobe** bio-compute

> An editor-assembled tour of how soft computing (fuzzy sets, GA/DE/PSO/ACO/BFOA, neural nets, rough sets, SVMs) and statistical pattern recognition get wired into concrete bioinformatics pipelines, from n-gram promoter recognition and miRNA target scoring through RNA and protein structure kernels, cheminformatics subgraph mining, de novo drug design, microarray clustering and gene-marker selection, to disease-gene prioritization and protein-interaction network mining.

## Reach for this when

- Turning a biological classification problem (interface vs non-interface residue, tumor vs normal sample, promoter vs non-promoter) into a supervised or unsupervised pipeline and picking the right CI primitive for it.
- Choosing a clustering algorithm plus validity index for gene-expression data and deciding between Jm, the I-index, silhouette s(C), and Xie-Beni.
- Doing feature or gene selection under a filter vs wrapper tradeoff, SNR ranking, or a multiobjective NSGA-II subset search.
- Designing a graph or subgraph kernel for molecules or proteins (optimal-assignment, pattern-diffusion, neighborhood/spectral kernels) to feed an SVM classifier.
- Prioritizing candidate disease genes from a linkage region using PPI networks with random-walk-with-restart, diffusion kernel, CIPHER, or ENDEAVOUR.
- Mining dense modules or quasi-cliques from a protein-protein interaction network and validating them with GO functional enrichment.
- Framing de novo ligand design as a variable-length GA over a fragment library docked against an active site.
- Wanting a worked catalog of evolutionary and swarm optimizers (DE, PSO, ACO, BFOA, GA, SA) mapped onto real biological objective functions rather than toy benchmarks.

## Principles

- Soft computing trades tolerance of imprecision for tractability and robustness; the book's spine is fuzzy sets (Zadeh, T-norm/T-conorm, center-of-gravity and mean-of-maxima defuzzification, Type-2 FOU), neural nets (MLP/RBF/SOM), evolutionary and swarm search (GA, DE with pbest/gbest-free difference operator, PSO with C1/C2, ACO stigmergy, BFOA chemotaxis), rough sets (Pawlak lower/upper approximation), and Pearl belief networks, each mapped to a bio task rather than left abstract.
- Clustering quality is never self-evident; it is measured by validity indices (Jm the FCM objective, the Maulik-Bandyopadhyay I-index built from 1/K, E1/EK and DK with power p=2, silhouette s(C) in [-1,1], and Xie-Beni), and the best algorithm is the one that optimizes these on real data, then confirmed by GO enrichment at hypergeometric p<0.01.
- Metaheuristic clustering beats local FCM: DEFC (CR=0.7, F=0.8, population 20, 100 generations) consistently outscored GAFC, SAFC, and FCM (yeast sporulation s(C) 0.5591 vs 0.5163 for FCM), and bolting a proximity-trained SVM onto the clusters lifts it further (0.5591 to 0.5797) because supervised beats unsupervised once labels are trusted.
- Kernels move biology into a linearly separable feature space: molecules become graphs mined for frequent subgraphs (gSpan, FSG, FFSM, GASTON, AGM) or scored by optimal-assignment (Hungarian algorithm) and pattern-diffusion kernels, and proteins become Protein Blocks (16 SOM-derived pentapeptide descriptors a-p, roughly 0.42 A RMSD) or SCOP neighborhood/spectral kernels using CE, DALI, and RMSD/dRMSD.
- Ensembles plus multiobjective selection cut cancer-classification variance: four SVM kernels (linear, polynomial, sigmoid, RBF) combined by majority voting over 200 solutions, then SNR ranking plus NSGA-II (accuracy vs gene-count) yields tiny marker sets (11 leukemia, 8 colon, 9 lymphoma genes) that outperform the full 100-gene set.
- Global network distance beats local for disease-gene ranking: random-walk-with-restart and diffusion kernels (Kohler) beat direct-interaction and shortest-path, with RWR mean enrichment 26 vs 18 for SP and DI, while CIPHER's Pearson-correlation-of-profiles handles the leave-all-out case with no known disease gene.
- Biological interaction networks are scale-free (P(k) ~ k^-gamma, Barabasi-Albert) and sparse (human HPRD clustering coefficient ~1.13E-4), so dense-module mining uses quasi-cliques and dense k-subnetworks with per-node association-density thresholds (delta=0.55) rather than strict cliques, because high-throughput data is noisy and biased toward positive interactions.

## Moves

- Promoter recognition (Ch4): n-gram features at n=2,3,4,5, EIIP numeric encoding, Bior3.3 wavelet transform, and cascaded NNPC/NNPN neural voting classifiers.
- miRNA target scoring (Ch5): miRanda-style Smith-Waterman complementarity with AU and GU wobble scoring, applied to prostate-cancer miRNA targets.
- RNA motif search (Ch6): Infernal covariance models (cmbuild/cmsearch/cmalign as profile SCFG), RSmatch O(mn) tree model with the RADAR server, Rfam (603 families, Stockholm seed and full alignments), RNAz, PhastCons phylo-HMM, and Vienna RNAfold/RNAcofold.
- Protein structure kernels (Ch7): neighborhood and spectral-projection kernels over the SCOP 40 percent nonredundant set using CE/DALI scores and RMSD/dRMSD, classified with LibSVM.
- Protein Blocks (Ch8): 16 structural prototypes a-p, pentapeptide fragments, 8 dihedral angles, RMSDA metric, SOM-derived, used for kinase activation-loop analysis.
- Cheminformatics (Ch9): frequent-subgraph mining (gSpan, FSG, FFSM, SPIN, GASTON, AGM), optimal-assignment kernel via Hungarian algorithm, GPD pattern-diffusion kernel (lambda=20 percent, tau=5), tested with LibSVM and Weka SMO.
- De novo drug design (Ch10): variable-length GA over a 41-fragment library, tree chromosome of 3 integers plus 12 pointers per node, active site mined on a 0.5 A grid with a 1.4 A water probe, Lennard-Jones 6-12 plus electrostatic fitness weighted between intra and inter energy (w=0.5 best), Srinivas-Patnaik adaptive crossover/mutation, targets HIV-1 protease (1AAQ, Ritonavir, RMSD 1.97 A) and thrombin (1DWD, NAPAP), benchmarked against NEWLEAD, LigBuilder, Insight II, and ConQuest/CSD.
- Microarray clustering (Ch11, Ch13): DEFC (DE difference mutation, CR=0.7, F=0.8) with FCM/GAFC/SAFC baselines, plus ParPSBKM parallel point-symmetry K-means in MPI/C on an IBM p690 Regatta with union-find inverse-Ackermann merge, reaching 8.06x speedup on the 44,291-gene colon-culture set.
- Gene-marker selection (Ch12): SVM four-kernel majority-vote ensemble, SNR statistic (mu over sigma per class), then NSGA-II (population 50, 100 generations, crossover 0.8, mutation 0.1) minimizing gene count while maximizing accuracy.
- Disease-gene prioritization and PPI mining (Ch14, Ch16): RWR (restart probability r, update p_{t+1} = r*p0 + (1-r)*W*p_t, converge at 1E-6), CIPHER Pearson-profile correlation, ENDEAVOUR ten-source Q-statistic fusion; dense k-subnetwork heuristic on HPRD (37,107 interactions across 25,661 proteins), SimCD/Czekanowski-Dice neighborhood weighting, delta=0.55, validated with FatiGO GO enrichment (p ~ 1E-05).

## Watch out

- This is a 2010 edited volume, so tool and database versions are dated (GALOPPS 3.2.4, Stuttgart Neural Network Simulator, Insight II/Accelrys, Rfam 603 families, HPRD 2009). Treat specific tool names as historical and verify current equivalents before use.
- Chapters are independent contributed papers with uneven rigor and clashing notation; there is no single unified method. It is a survey and catalog, not a textbook to implement front to back.
- Most results are self-comparisons on a handful of benchmark datasets (yeast sporulation, leukemia/colon/lymphoma, four or five microarrays), so the recurring 'our method wins' claims lack broad external validation and are leads to verify, not settled facts.
- The extracted text garbles nearly all equations, figures, and tables (formulas render as bare placeholders like (11.2)), so numeric parameters stated in prose are usable but derived formulas must be reconstructed from the cited primary papers.
- PPI and interactome findings inherit high-throughput noise and a positive-interaction bias that the book itself flags (missing negative-interaction data); the topological modules are candidate complexes, not validated ones.
- Small-sample cancer classification (72, 62, and 96 samples over 100 genes) is prone to overfitting, so the reported marker gene sets need independent-cohort and biological confirmation before trust.
