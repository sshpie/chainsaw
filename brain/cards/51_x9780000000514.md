# 51. Cyberjutsu: Cybersecurity for the Modern Ninja

**ISBN** x9780000000514  |  **Lobe** defensive

> McCarty's chapter-per-tactic translation of the medieval ninja scrolls into modern defense, each pairing a Bansenshukai passage with a Castle Theory thought exercise and NIST 800-53 controls, teaching us to model, sense, deceive, and attribute a patient adversary by thinking like the original advanced persistent threat.

## Reach for this when

- We are designing detection placement for an AI-infra sweep and want the sensor doctrine: most orgs trust one egress sensor, the shinobi answer is many cheap inward-facing sensors along the likely path (Chapter 9), the mental model for where aimap/scanner liveness probes will and will not be seen
- We are reasoning about operator attribution on a fingerprinted fleet and need the counter-attribution discipline: the art of the fireflies (Chapter 15) is the warning that strings, infra, and artifacts can be deliberately planted to push a false narrative, plus the three honest options (shed bias, build attribution capability, or forget attribution)
- We are deciding whether a candidate is a real exposure or bait before we name a finding: Moon on the Water (Chapter 12) and Live Capture (Chapter 16) are the honeypot-and-lure lens for the LBot deception-fleet problem and the SYN-ACK-only ambiguity
- We are writing a finding for a non-technical CISO or stakeholder and want the Castle Theory framing: strip the jargon, defend a castle from ninjas, get clear communication and a fixable recommendation rather than a CVE dump
- We are scoping a defender's blind spots during recon and want the offense-informs-defense map: network mapping (Chapter 1), boundary bridging (Chapter 10), hours of infiltration (Chapter 6) and litter discipline (Chapter 20) name exactly the gaps an APT exploits
- We need to reason about our own footprint and detectability during an authorized engagement: Light, Noise, and Litter Discipline (Chapter 20) is the OPSEC primer on the traces we leave and what a watchful defender would catch

## Principles

- The shinobi were the first advanced persistent threat: the deepest principle of ninjutsu, avoid where the enemy is attentive and strike where he is negligent, is the patient-aggregation, recon-first adversary model that every chapter reverse-engineers into a defense
- Defense is built by thinking like the attacker, then inverting: each chapter reads an infiltration technique from the scrolls and asks what control would have stopped it, the offense-informs-defense loop made literal across 26 topics
- Uniqueness beats universal standards: xenophobic security (Chapter 3) argues interoperability and shared defaults are the attack surface, custom and heterogeneous environments (anti-privilege, custom protections, the disconnect skill) frustrate an adversary who trained on the standard
- Attribution is hard, deliberately poisoned, and often unnecessary: the art of the fireflies shows artifacts can be planted to misattribute, and most orgs should defend against the threat behavior rather than chase the actor's identity
- Communication is a security control: the Castle Theory thought exercise removes technical jargon so nontechnical stakeholders can reason about the real risk, because many security failures are human and political, not technical
