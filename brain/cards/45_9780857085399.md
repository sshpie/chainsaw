# 45. The Art of Conversation

**ISBN** 9780857085399  |  **Lobe** human

> A state-first elicitation and rapport toolkit: connection-over-content conversation mechanics you reach for when the target, asset, or gatekeeper is a human, not a host.

## Reach for this when

- Running a vishing or pretext call and you need the operator to keep talking, fill silence, and volunteer detail without sensing the pull (the matching-then-leading and 'positive sensor' moves carry the elicitation).
- Writing or delivering a disclosure to a defensive vendor SOC/CISO who can react with shame or denial: connection-over-content, win-win confrontation script, and 'assume positive intention' reframe the email from accusation to collaboration.
- Interviewing or shadowing a plant operator / SCADA tech to map an OT environment, where the real attack surface is the undocumented workaround they will only mention if they feel heard, not interrogated.
- Managing a live engagement client call or scope negotiation where your own social fear or over-talking is leaking and degrading the room (state-management drills + 'stop, breathe, speak low and slow').
- Designing a social-engineering test plan or pretext and you need a vocabulary for the rapport mechanics you are weaponizing, plus a built-in tripwire for when faked connection gets detected.
- Calibrating an LLM conversational agent or chat-based recon persona: the talk-type ladder and 'message beneath the message' give concrete state to encode and a Turing-failure tell ('a few words before you know it's a machine').

## Principles

- Connection beats content. Successful exchanges blend both 'in varying proportions, but connection is always key' - the words are the cover, rapport is the channel.
- Your inner state is the load-bearing variable: 'more than skill, voice, fluency or intelligence, good conversation depends to an enormous extent on your state of mind.' Manage your state before you manage theirs.
- Listening is more than half the job and the rarest skill: most people rehearse, filter, judge, or play psychiatrist instead. Deep listening hears 'the message beneath the message' - the operational gold in any elicitation.
- Match then lead: mirror body language, voice (speed/volume/pitch/range), and underlying energy first, then steer. Match the energy, not the mood. Strong values, flexible behavior (the tree metaphor).
- Meaning lives in the deep structure (voice, breathing, pauses, body), not the dictionary - 'words don't have fixed meanings.' How it is said outweighs what is said.
- Faked connection to manipulate gets detected and breeds distrust (the flatmate's phone-call tell). The book's own ethics tripwire: the same mechanics that build rapport leave a signature when forced.

## Moves

- Drains diagnostic: spot the six archetypes (Enthuso-bore, Robo-chore, Echo-bore, Ego-bore, Queen of Gossip, Moaning Mickey/Minnie) in a gatekeeper, then choose to join their game for rapport or break connection to release yourself. Spot them in yourself on a client call too.
- Talk-type ladder as an elicitation depth gauge: Thing -> Action -> Head -> Heart -> Soul. Climb deliberately ('a more personal how', 'What do you enjoy about it?', 'Who are you when...?') to move an operator from facts to the candid stuff; gracefully stay put if they don't follow.
- State drills before a high-stakes call: deep breath before entering and when stuck; unobtrusive ankle/knee/hand movement; collect a positive state in full sensory muscle memory; focus five senses externally to kill the self-conscious loop. 'Tense body = blocked mind.'
- Keep-it-going mechanics: conversation as tennis - follow every answer with a return question, never give bald monosyllables ('nails in the coffin'), 'add a little extra' each turn, use open what/how questions, pave the way before changing subject.
- Positive-sensor questioning: replace backward 'why did this happen?' with forward 'where from here?'; convert NEG statements to POS questions; deploy presupposition-loaded 'if' questions that assume the outcome you want.
- Six-step I-language confrontation script for the hard disclosure or scope dispute: identify the issue, voice your perception, invite their side, stay firm, say what you want, check agreement - paired with 'assume positive intention, assume success.'
- Game counter-moves from Johnstone (status) and Berne (parent/adult/child, 'Yes but'): refuse the low-status role offered, handle a pitch with 'agreement without being convinced', and 'name the game' / name the elephant to unblock a stuck or hidden-emotion exchange.

## Watch out

- Practitioner workbook, not research. NLP/coaching lineage (Apps is ICF PCC and an NLP trainer); the mirroring/energetic-connection and especially Soul Talk material is lightly evidenced and leans spiritual-experiential. Treat moves as field heuristics to A/B test, not validated effects.
- The mechanics are dual-use and the book knows it. Matching-then-leading IS elicitation tradecraft; using it on a person outside engagement scope is manipulation. The 'faked connection gets detected' tell is also your own exposure - forced rapport leaves a signature a wary target will read.
- Cultural and channel calibration: mirroring norms, eye-contact and 45-degree-chair cues, and energy-matching are Western-conversational defaults. They do not transfer cleanly across cultures, over phone/text-only vishing, or to neurodivergent counterparties.
- 'Connection over content' is wrong for the technical core of the work. It governs the human channel only - never let rapport-thinking soften a finding write-up, a CVSS justification, or a verification step. Content discipline still wins the report.
- Soul Talk / 'who are you when...' depth-laddering on a live target can overreach into something coercive or simply blow the pretext by feeling invasive. Climb the ladder slowly and read the trap icon: the book itself flags faked depth as a drain.
