# 13. Human Factors in Cybersecurity

**ISBN** 9781806118335  |  **Lobe** defensive

> The causal-grammar tool for explaining WHY a misconfig or a clicked link recurs - HFACS four levels plus nanocodes turn "human error" from a blame verdict into a designable, frequency-counted system property you can chart and fix.

## Reach for this when

- Writing the root-cause / remediation section of a report and you need to push past 'user clicked' to the latent organizational and design conditions a defender can actually fix - HFACS gives the four-level walkout (Unsafe Act -> Precondition -> Supervision -> Organizational Influence).
- Modeling a social-engineering chain (phishing, vishing, whaling, BEC, fake-employee) and you want the cognitive mechanism, not just the lure: urgency/authority/familiarity heuristics, Cialdini levers, Yerkes-Dodson arousal, online disinhibition.
- Designing or critiquing adaptive auth / step-up friction and you need a principled cue-set with explicit weights and thresholds instead of uniform controls (impossible travel +3, new device +2, repeated MFA failures +3).
- Assessing a SOC's effective detection capacity, where alert fatigue, learned helplessness, automation bias, and a 45-90 second single-screen attention span are the real false-negative drivers - the human side of why the scanner-equivalent misses things.
- Designing human-AI-teaming or agentic-orchestration guardrails: calibrated trust, out-of-the-loop problem, automation bias vs distrust, black-box opacity, and explainability (LIME, confidence scoring) as the human-factors mirror of verification-as-load-bearing-stage.
- Building a defender-empathy / business-context frame for a CISO so the narrative report names the systemic condition and the fix, not a scapegoat.

## Principles

- Human error is a symptom, not a root cause - Reason's Swiss-cheese model: trace from the visible unsafe act outward through preconditions, supervision, and organizational influences (latent vs active failures).
- Make the secure action the easiest action - conserve scarce human attention (Cowan's magical-number-4, not 7+-2; vigilance decrements in minutes) for genuinely high-risk decisions; uniform friction breeds shadow security.
- A just / blame-free culture is a control, not a nicety - fear of punishment suppresses early reporting and weak-signal disclosure, directly degrading detection and containment speed.
- Burnout is operational risk on the register, not an HR footnote - fatigue measurably raises false-negative rates and attrition; instrument it (MBI, PSS, HRV, CyFa, shift logs).
- Behavior change is engineered systems first, role-specific training second, campaigns third - awareness alone rarely moves behavior at scale (Chadwick 2023: two-thirds know weak-password risk, a minority act).
- Measurement is make-or-break - mixed-methods human-system KPIs (decision quality, workload balance, time-to-contain, tool pivots, reopened-ticket rate, pulse-survey fatigue) or the program dies on un-quantifiable claims.

## Moves

- HFACS-Cyber root-cause walkout: code an incident across all four levels (Unsafe Acts: slips/lapses/mistakes/violations; Preconditions; Supervisory Factors; Organizational Influences), then write the remediation against the deepest layer you can name, not the click.
- Build organization-specific nanocodes: granular alphanumeric sub-codes (model AD005 Inadequate Real-Time Assessment, PC004 Miscommunication) mined bottom-up from AARs/near-misses, frequency-counted, so cross-case trend charts drive prioritized systemic fixes instead of one-off blame.
- Weighted heuristic cue set: pick a small empirically-useful signal set, assign explicit risk weights (impossible travel +3, new device +2, unusual proxy +2, first-time country +2, repeated MFA failures +3), set action thresholds and scope, revalidate monthly against TP/FP rates and attacker drift.
- Task analysis then cognitive workload analysis: map one bounded scenario (the 2:15 a.m. impossible-travel SOC case) as performed, annotate workload drivers, measure three ways (subjective NASA-TLX; behavioral time-to-decision/errors/rework; operational telemetry of queue depth, tool pivots, paging frequency), fix where overload changes the outcome, re-measure.
- Dark-triad offender profiling for attribution narrative: Machiavellianism / narcissism / psychopathy as salient cyber-offending predictors when characterizing an adversary or insider-threat actor.
- Apply COM-B (Capability, Opportunity, Motivation -> Behavior) to diagnose why a secure-behavior intervention failed - is the gap capability, opportunity, or motivation, before you blame the human.
- Run a minimal-viable HFACS-Cyber loop: define domain nanocodes, code incidents/near-misses consistently, quarterly translate top findings into engineered interventions, track strain alongside performance.

## Watch out

- Broad, repetitive, and citation-padded with occasional AI-generated-feel prose and few hard novel datasets - the extractable value is the named frameworks and step-procedures, not deep technical detail. Do not expect exploit primitives.
- HFACS has candid built-in weaknesses the book itself names: no inherent quantification, retrospective/hindsight bias, and inter-rater reliability problems. Two analysts can code the same incident differently - pair nanocodes with FMEA/STAMP-STPA/FRAM/Bayesian nets when you need rigor.
- Aviation-template borrowing is the book's strength and its blind spot - safety-critical aviation has tight regulation and standardized roles that most security orgs lack, so transplanted CRM/just-culture practice needs adaptation, not copy-paste.
- HFEaaS (the service-model chapter) is aspirational with admittedly immature ROI metrics and provider-dependence/data-privacy risks - treat it as a hiring/partnering argument, not a validated market category.
- The cyberpsychology chapter doubles as an offensive toolkit (heuristic exploitation, dark-triad profiling, disinformation cascades) - in a defensive-lobe book; keep the framing honest about which moves are attack-modeling vs defense.
- Statistics are cited not generated (45-90s attention span, sub-1%-of-users / 80%-of-fake-news-exposure, MBI dimensions) - verify against primary sources before quoting in a report; the book is a pointer, not the dataset.
