# 64. Evolutionary Computation with Biogeography-based Optimization

**ISBN** 9781848218079  |  **Lobe** bio-compute

> The definitive single-algorithm treatment of biogeography-based optimization: migration/mutation operators built from island-biogeography HSI/SIV theory, three exact analytical models (Markov, dynamic-system, statistical-mechanics) with elitism convergence proofs, and the full extension zoo (combinatorial, constrained, noisy/Kalman, multi-objective, hybrid) benchmarked head-to-head against GA/PSO/DE/ACO/ES.

## Reach for this when

- You want a population metaheuristic whose solutions persist across generations and improve by feature-sharing (migration) instead of being discarded by crossover; basic BBO beat ACO/DE/ES/GA and tied/edged PSO on 9 of 13 standard benchmarks (Table 3.3).
- You must prove an EA converges or does not: Ch5 gives the Markov-chain construction, the Perron-Frobenius/primitive-matrix argument, and the pair of theorems that BBO without elitism never converges (Thm 5.6) but elite BBO does (Thm 5.7), with a rho-based rate bound (Thm 5.8).
- You want exact analytical predictions instead of unreliable Monte Carlo: pick Markov (steady-state distribution over all populations), dynamic-system (proportion of each solution as N approaches infinity), or statistical-mechanics (cumulant kappa1/kappa2/kappa3 trajectories) by problem size.
- You are tuning an EA and need concrete guidance: mutation-rate sweep Pm = 0.1/0.01/0.001 (lower gives faster convergence, Tables 5.3-5.5), population sweet spot N = 50 (Table 3.4), migration-curve shape, and E=I=1 normalization.
- You are solving a combinatorial problem (TSP, graph coloring, 0/1 knapsack, MST) and need order/cycle/inver-over/matrix migration plus 2-opt inversion, insertion, displacement, and reciprocal-exchange mutation with nearest-neighbor initialization.
- You have a constrained problem and must choose among eight constraint-handling methods (static/dynamic/adaptive penalty, superiority-of-feasible, eclectic, niched-penalty, stochastic ranking, epsilon-level) benchmarked on the 2006 and 2010 CEC sets.
- Your fitness function is noisy and you need re-sampling (variance down by factor iota) or a scalar Kalman-filter fitness estimator so mis-ranked solutions do not corrupt the migration ranking.
- You have conflicting objectives and want Pareto machinery (domination, hypervolume) wired into BBO via VEBBO/NSBBO/NPBBO/SPBBO, or you want to hybridize BBO with GA/PSO/DE at the iteration level or the algorithm level.

## Principles

- HSI/SIV mapping is the whole analogy. A candidate solution is a habitat; its decision variables are suitability index variables (SIVs); solution quality is the habitat suitability index (HSI). Good solutions resist change (low immigration lambda, high emigration mu); poor solutions accept features (high lambda, low mu).
- Migration is feature-sharing, not reproduction. Immigration rate lambda_k decides whether SIV s of solution k is replaced; roulette-wheel over emigration rates {mu_j} (Eq 3.1) picks the donor. Unlike GA/ES, BBO solutions are modified in place and survive generations; unlike PSO/DE they change directly, with no velocity and no difference vector.
- BBO generalizes a specific GA: set lambda_k = 1 for all k and BBO reduces exactly to a genetic algorithm with global uniform recombination (GA/GUR). GA/GUR is the anchor against which every Markov, dynamic-system, and statistical-mechanics comparison in the book is drawn.
- Nonlinear and blended migration beat the linear baseline. Quadratic, sinusoidal, and generalized-sinusoidal curves (negative offset beta = -pi/2 gives temporary positive immigration feedback) plus blended migration (new SIV = alpha*own + (1-alpha)*donor, alpha = 0.5 best) improve results; emigration-based variants win on unimodal functions, immigration-based on multimodal (Table 4.3).
- Elitism is what makes convergence provable. Without elitism the transition matrix is primitive, giving a unique positive limiting distribution, so the global optimum is never held (Thm 5.6). Preserve one elite and the a*-chain gains an absorbing-state block [I 0; R Q] (Thm 5.7), guaranteeing global convergence; the rate improves as rho = ||Q||_inf shrinks, i.e., as mutation rate drops.
- Three exact models, three regimes. Markov gives steady-state over all populations but explodes with cardinality (T = C(n+N-1, N); a 3-bit N=4 problem is already 330x330). Dynamic-system cuts states to n (population to infinity, deterministic). Statistical-mechanics tracks only the first three cumulants of the fitness distribution and survives large, separable, high-dimensional problems.
- BBO is close to GA in dynamics but distinct in mechanism. The statistical-mechanics equations differ only by a (1 - kappa1/(n+1)) factor that vanishes as n grows; yet at the low mutation rates typical of real problems, BBO's dynamic-system steady-state beats GA/GUR and GA/SP by orders of magnitude on hard needle and deceptive functions (Tables 6.1-6.2, best on 13 of 16).
- Noise corrupts the ranking that drives migration. Because lambda and mu come from fitness order, additive-Gaussian mis-ranking inverts emigration/immigration and breaks migration; re-sampling iota times cuts fitness-estimate variance by iota, and scalar Kalman filtering (P0 = infinity, recursive update Eq 10.11) recovers near-noiseless performance (Table 10.1).

## Moves

- Basic BBO loop (Fig 3.5): copy population {x} to temp {z}; per solution test lambda_k against rand(0,1) to select immigration; per selected SIV run roulette-wheel over {mu_j} (Eq 3.1) to choose the donor; mutate each SIV with probability Pm to a uniform rand(Ls,Us); recompute HSI; keep 2 elites. Normalize E = I = 1, Pm = 0.01, N = 50.
- Migration-curve menu: linear lambda = 1 - r, mu = r over fitness rank r in [0,1]; quadratic (Eq 4.2); sinusoidal (Eq 4.3); generalized sinusoidal with beta = -pi/2 for temporary positive immigration feedback (Eq 4.4); layer blended migration z(SIV) = alpha*xk(SIV) + (1-alpha)*xj(SIV) with alpha = 0.5, and pick partial/total x immigration/emigration per problem modality.
- Prove convergence: build the Markov transition matrix Q from the generalized multinomial theorem (Eq 5.48) over all T populations (T = C(n+N-1, N) from Eq 5.19); confirm primitivity (Thm 5.5); for elite BBO factor the a*-chain matrix into [I 0; R Q] (Eq 5.61) and bound time-to-optimum t from rho = ||Q||_inf via Eq 5.66. Lower mutation rate lowers rho and speeds convergence.
- Statistical-mechanics pre-tuning for expensive fitness: iterate the three-cumulant map (Eq 7.43) with kappa4 set to 0 to predict kappa1 (mean) rising and kappa2 (variance) falling; use it to choose population size and mutation rate before spending real evaluations, and correct for finite N with the sample-cumulant factors (Eq 7.46).
- TSP: nearest-neighbor init (Sec 8.2.1); path representation; order/cycle/inver-over migration or matrix migration (n x n precedence matrix, intersection = elementwise AND, union = OR plus a cut-point repair); mutate by inversion (2-opt), insertion, displacement, or reciprocal exchange; grow k in k-opt with generation count (Eq 8.17). Att-48 best tour about 44,598 (order migration + inversion).
- Graph coloring: store an ordered vertex list per habitat, share order via inver-over migration, color with the greedy next-available-color-not-used-by-a-neighbor algorithm (Fig 8.9); cost = number of colors, which drives lambda and mu. Beat GA/GUR on Leighton (450 v, 17343 e), Mycielski, and Flat benchmarks.
- Constrained BBO: convert equality h(x) = 0 to |h(x)| <= epsilon (epsilon about 1e-4, shrink over generations); rank with a penalized cost (Eq 9.5); prefer exponential-dynamic-penalty (EP, alpha about 10), adaptive-penalty (AP: beta1 = 4, beta2 = 3, k = n), or niched-penalty (NP tournament) since those three won most CEC 2006/2010 functions; define the elite as the best feasible solution so feasibility is never lost.
- Noisy fitness: re-sample iota = 5 and average (variance falls by iota, Eq 10.9), or run scalar Kalman BBO (init P0 = infinity so f_hat_1 = g1; update f_hat and P via Eq 10.11) and re-evaluate the highest-uncertainty solution among those above one standard deviation below the mean (Stroud rule Eq 10.15); split NFFEs as fraction F new solutions, (1-F) re-evaluations.
- Multi-objective BBO: replace fitness with domination-based cost and wire BBO migration into the classic MOEAs, giving VEBBO (from VEGA), NSBBO (from NSGA/NSGA-II, crowding distance Eq 11.16), NPBBO (from NPGA), and SPBBO (from SPEA: strength value plus archive clustering); score with hypervolume and normalized hypervolume; SPBBO won most CEC-2009 MOP benchmarks and a real automated-warehouse scheduling MOP.
- Hybridize: opposition-based BBO (reflected/quasi/super/quasi-reflected opposite, jump rate Jr = 0.3, quasi-reflected best, Table 12.1); add local search (gradient descent > boundary > Latin hypercube > grid in importance, activate at 50% of NFFEs when best improves < 10% generation to generation); iteration-level (run an EA then BBO) or algorithm-level (use BBO migration to exchange info among subpopulations) hybridization, where algorithm-level > iteration-level > the constituent EA (Tables 12.3-12.4).

## Watch out

- Math-heavy, and the extraction is lossy. Ch5-7 (Markov, dynamic-system, statistical-mechanics) are dense with matrix theorems, multinomial combinatorics, and cumulant generating functions; the equations are referenced by number but their bodies are frequently stripped in this text dump, so you must recompute or consult the original ISTE/Wiley edition (Ma and Simon).
- The exact models are teaching tools, not production analysis. The Markov matrix is T x T with T = C(n+N-1, N); a trivial 3-bit N=4 problem already needs 330x330, so anything realistic is analytically intractable and the models serve only as small-problem sanity checks or parameter-tuning approximations.
- Author-favorable benchmarking. Comparisons deliberately use basic versions of GA/PSO/DE/ACO/ES for fairness, and the book concedes tuned competitors could win; do not read Table 3.3's 9-of-13 as BBO dominance in the wild. On real-world problems (Table 4.5) BBO merely matched ADE and beat SGA/SPSO, not a knockout.
- No runnable code, one author lineage. Everything traces to Dan Simon's BBO program of research (the SIM and MA citations); you get pseudo-code outlines only (Fig 3.5 and kin), so you build the implementation yourself and the MATLAB reference lives off-page.
- Narrow by design. This is one algorithm in depth, not a survey; for the wider metaheuristic landscape and engineering applications you need Book 62, and for the medical-data-mining application layer Book 65.
- Biogeography-is-optimization is a thesis, not a theorem. Ch2's argument that nature optimizes (Krakatoa, Amazon, Gaia-flavored citations) is philosophical motivation; the engineering payoff rests on the benchmarks, not on the biogeography metaphysics, so keep the two separate when you cite it.
