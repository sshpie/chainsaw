# 14. Persuade

**ISBN** 9781119778516  |  **Lobe** human

> A sequenced influence-without-authority state machine (Credibility to Emotion to Logic to Action, wrapped in timing) you reach for when a finding has to move someone who owes you nothing, and a catalog of the exact cognitive levers a social-engineering adversary will pull.

## Reach for this when

- Pushing a finding to a CERT, vendor PSIRT, or government body that owes you nothing - the triager accepts or rejects on who-it-is-from and how-it-is-framed BEFORE the technical merits land, which is the whole thesis.
- Writing a disclosure report or bug-bounty writeup that has to change a decision, not just document one - sequence credibility/emotion/logic instead of leading with the CVE dump.
- Modeling or building a social-engineering attack (phishing, pretext, vishing) and you need the named primitives an adversary uses: altercasting, small-yes ladder, manufactured urgency, negative social proof.
- Designing or red-teaming an agentic-AI negotiation/communication flow - this is a persuasion state machine with explicit preconditions, useful as a template and as an exploit catalog.
- Defending against manipulation: you need a vocabulary (the five ethical tenets, white-hat/black-hat line) to name an influence operation as an attack surface.
- Negotiating engagement scope, budget, or remediation timelines with a client who cares about different things than you do.

## Principles

- Strict dependency order is the differentiator, not the tactics: you cannot engage emotion before credibility, cannot land logic before emotion, cannot facilitate action before all three. A correct tactic in the wrong layer fails.
- Decisions are made emotionally and justified rationally; logic's real job is to let people justify a choice they already felt. Leading with logic is the single most common failure - the dark-mirror version is why a technically perfect report still gets ignored.
- Credibility = Trust + Expertise, and expertise must be domain-relevant (cashier vs Johns Hopkins internist on blueberries). General trustworthiness does not transfer to a specific claim - your CVE history buys you nothing on a domain you have not published in.
- Vulnerability precedes trust rather than following it: statements against self-interest, sharing weakness, asking for help trigger a vulnerability loop. Caveat - the authors' OWN study failed to confirm statements-against-self-interest raise trust.
- Framing and defaults are quietly decisive and do not wash out with expertise: loss-framing drove a 50%+ valuation swing ($176,284 vs $94,472); opt-out moved organ-donor registration from ~15% to >90%. These are the levers an adversary sets and a defender must audit.
- Timing (Kairos) is a first-class variable: parole odds collapse to ~0% before lunch vs ~65% after eating; morning = careful, night = creative. When you send the report matters as much as what it says.

## Moves

- Identify your lab coats: borrow credibility before the ask (Nathan's paid doctors in lab coats). For disclosure - lead with the CVE/advisory track record, the CERT relationship, the trustmark, NOT the payload. Ask 'who are my lab coats?' before sending.
- Score the relationship with the Trust Equation (Connection + Value + Reliability over Self-interest, 0-15) and the Trusted Advisor Quick Score (0-20) to diagnose WHY a triager is stonewalling before you escalate.
- Use the If/When-Then-Because formula and never drop 'because' - Langer's copier study took compliance 60% to 93-94% even with an empty reason. Caveat - a BAD reason underperforms NO reason, so the justification still has to hold.
- Give 3-5 options, never 1 and never 24: single-option aversion (Mochon DVD 9-10% vs 66%) kills decisions, choice overload (Iyengar jam 30% vs 3%) paralyzes. For remediation - offer 3 fix paths with a sensible default, not one mandate or a menu of twelve.
- Run the close taxonomy deliberately: soft closes (question, trial) under the 70/30 listening rule first, hard closes (summary, direct, urgency, assumed) only when warranted; get a Small Yes first to ride commitment/consistency bias.
- Read the room with Ekman's seven universal microexpressions and the 50/70 eye-contact rule; mirror verbal (rate, vocabulary, name) and nonverbal - but watch culture (Jeff's Nepal name-repetition backfire).
- Profile with OCEAN/Big Five and switch the script: give Open people exactly three high-level options, give Conscientious people the agenda and all details up front, persuade Agreeable people by persuading those around them.
- Time the send to the cognitive task and the forgetting curve; engineer a happy ending (peak-end rule) so the last interaction is the one remembered.

## Watch out

- It is a business/sales trade book, not rigorous science. Do not reuse its numbers as load-bearing facts in a finding.
- Multiple cited claims are contested or overstated: power posing (Cuddy) failed to replicate, the Mehrabian 55%-body-language figure is misapplied, and the precise 22x storytelling multiplier (Bruner) is soft. Flag any of these if quoted.
- The authors' OWN MTurk study (827 responses, hypothetical scenarios) is survey work, not field validation - and it flatly FAILED to confirm two of its own marquee tactics: statements against self-interest did not raise trust, and the safety-net opt-out did not raise satisfaction; small-yesses only partially supported.
- The white-hat framing is thin. Every technique here is a dual-use social-engineering primitive; treat the book as an adversary's playbook as much as a persuader's, and do not let the five-tenets ethics gloss obscure that.
- Mirroring and microexpression reads are culturally bound (the Nepal name-repetition backfire); a tactic that builds rapport in one context insults in another. Do not apply the scripts blind across cultures.
